# Language as a supervisory or calibration signal for unsupervised decoder adaptation

Scope: work that uses language models, text statistics or task structure in place of labeled calibration to train or recalibrate an input decoder (BCI, EMG/IMU gesture, keyboard side channel), including work that resolves an unknown cluster-to-symbol mapping. The goal is to judge relevance to our proposal: a 4-channel piezo wristband whose post-rewear stroke clusters (~93% purity, a project figure, not from the literature) need a direction label (UP/DOWN/LEFT/RIGHT, 4! = 24 permutations). The language model would pick the permutation and then supply pseudo-labels for continuous recalibration.

Search date: 2026-10-17. Verification legend: "verified" means I checked the primary source (paper PDF text or publisher/ACL/NeurIPS abstract page) in this session. "Abstract-level" means I saw only the abstract or a search-index summary. "Unverified" means I recall it but did not check it in this session.

## Q1. ERP/P300 spellers: unsupervised EM plus language models (Kindermans, Verstraeten, Schrauwen), LLP (Hübner), MIX (Verhoeven), follow-ups

### Takeaway
The Ghent/Berlin line showed that a P300 speller can be trained with no labels at all ("zero-training"). It works because the paradigm forces structure: exactly one row and one column contain the target. A character n-gram LM adds a prior that mostly shortens the warm-up period in which output is random. Learning from label proportions (LLP) later added a guarantee: 84.5% of characters correct online with no calibration (N=13). This is the cleanest precedent for "language removes calibration". But the unknown there is a binary target/non-target classifier constrained by the stimulus design. It is not an arbitrary permutation of symbol clusters.

### Cited Findings
- Kindermans, Verstraeten & Schrauwen (2012), "A Bayesian Model for Exploiting Application Constraints to Enable Unsupervised Training of a P300-based BCI", PLoS ONE 7(4): e33758, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0033758. The classifier is "trained entirely unsupervisedly using an Expectation Maximization approach", and on public datasets it was competitive with supervised methods (abstract-level). Note: the title the caller gave ("A Bayesian model to combine unsupervised learning and language models...") does not match this published title. The PLoS paper's title says "application constraints". — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0033758.html)
- Kindermans, Verschore, Verstraeten & Schrauwen (2012), "A P300 BCI for the Masses: Prior Information Enables Instant Unsupervised Spelling", NIPS 2012 (verified from the PDF text). It uses an unsupervised hierarchical probabilistic model with (i) transfer learning from other subjects and (ii) uni/bi/trigram character LMs (Witten-Bell smoothing, trained on 5·10^8 characters) placed in the graphical model as the prior over the spelled character. Data: the Akimpech dataset, 22 subjects, re-synthesized to spell realistic English-like text. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c042f4db68f23406c6cecf84a7ebb0fe-Paper.pdf)
  - Reported numbers (online spelling accuracy, averaged over subjects). Plain unsupervised (RA−): 24.6% at 3 epochs and 82.1% at 15 epochs. Re-testing the final classifier after the session: 60.5% (3 epochs) and 94.6% (15 epochs), which shows a warm-up period. Adding a trigram LM: 43.5% vs 24.6% at 3 epochs. The authors conclude "the language model mainly helps to reduce the warm up period". The full model (transfer + adaptation + trigram, TA_tri) reached 73.8% at 3 epochs, then 82.1 / 87.0 / 95.0 / 97.9% at 4/5/10/15 epochs (Table 1, TA*_tri column as extracted). It "parallels and in some cases even surpasses" supervised BLDA. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c042f4db68f23406c6cecf84a7ebb0fe-Paper.pdf)
  - The paper also notes that without priors the unsupervised classifier is "randomly initialized", and that the remedy of "evaluating many random initializations and selecting the best" is suboptimal. This is the ERP analog of our permutation search. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c042f4db68f23406c6cecf84a7ebb0fe-Paper.pdf)
- The NeurIPS listing confirms the venue and the abstract: "performance of the unsupervised models parallels and in some cases even surpasses that of supervised models". — [NeurIPS virtual poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2012/poster/3516)
- A Kindermans et al. 2014 Journal of Neural Engineering paper (DOI 10.1088/1741-2560/11/3/035005) integrates dynamic stopping, transfer learning and language models in one probabilistic framework ("harvests structure ... by transfer learning, unsupervised adaptation, language models, and dynamic stopping"). Only the search-result abstract was seen; the exact title is unverified. — [DOI link](https://www.doi.org/10.1088/1741-2560/11/3/035005)
- A 2014 PLoS ONE paper (record pone.0102504) appears in the same results. It is very likely Kindermans, Schreuder, Schrauwen, Müller & Tangermann, "True zero-training brain-computer interfacing – an online study", PLoS ONE 9(7): e102504. The title and authors are unverified; I did not open the record. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0102504.html)
- Hübner, Verhoeven, Schmid, Müller, Tangermann & Kindermans (2017), "Learning from label proportions in brain-computer interfaces: Online unsupervised learning with guarantees", PLoS ONE (arXiv:1701.07213). The paradigm was modified so that stimulus groups have known target/non-target proportions, which lets LLP recover the class means. Online: 84.5% of characters correct with no prior calibration, N=13. Described as "the first unsupervised decoder for ERP BCIs guaranteed to find the true class means". Data on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.192684). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07213); [PLoS/DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/74364237147145a8bbe830792f529ed7)
- Verhoeven et al. (2017), "Improving zero-training brain-computer interfaces by mixing model estimators", J. Neural Eng. (MIX = combining the EM and LLP estimators). Abstract-level, via search index. — [Korea Univ. record](https://pure.korea.ac.kr/en/publications/improving-zero-training-brain-computer-interfaces-by-mixing-model/)
- Hübner, Verhoeven, Müller, Kindermans & Tangermann (2018), "Unsupervised Learning for Brain-Computer Interfaces Based on Event-Related Potentials: Review and Online Comparison", IEEE Computational Intelligence Magazine 13(2):66–77. It reviews and compares EM, LLP and MIX online and argues for "learning from unlabeled data gained from actual BCI usage instead of conducting calibration recordings". Abstract-level. — [Korea Univ. record](https://pure.korea.ac.kr/en/publications/unsupervised-learning-for-brain-computer-interfaces-based-on-even/); [Radboud lab page](https://neurotechlab.socsci.ru.nl/publication/hue-ver-mue-kin-tan-18)
- Follow-up: "UMM: Unsupervised Mean-difference Maximization" (arXiv:2306.11830, 2023) continues calibration-free ERP decoding. Seen only as a search hit; contents unverified. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.11830)
- Code: Hübner's Unsupervised-BCI-Matlab repository. — [GitHub](https://github.com/DavidHuebner/Unsupervised-BCI-Matlab)

### Inferences
- In ERP spellers the language model is a prior on the latent character, and that character deterministically fixes which stimuli were targets. Our case has the same structure: the latent letter or word fixes the direction label of each stroke. So the EM-with-LM graphical model carries over directly. Our 4! = 24 permutation is a discrete global latent variable on top of it, and it can be marginalized exactly (24 hypotheses are cheap).
- The ERP lesson that "the LM mainly shortens the warm-up" suggests what to measure: words or strokes needed until the posterior over the 24 permutations concentrates, not only final accuracy.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact titles and numbers of the 2014 J Neural Eng and PLoS ONE e102504 papers, or the per-subject results of MIX and the 2018 CIM paper.
- I found no ERP paper that faces a symbol permutation ambiguity. Its symmetry is resolved by the stimulus design, not by the LM.

## Q2. Intracortical/speech BCIs: language- or task-derived pseudo-labels and unsupervised alignment

### Takeaway
There are two strands. (1) Task- or language-inferred pseudo-labels for recalibration: retrospective target inference (Jarosiewicz 2015); CORP (Fan, Willett et al., NeurIPS 2023), which kept an online handwriting iBCI at 93.84% over 403 days; and B-CORP (Cowan & Linderman, NeurIPS 2026, soft top-K LM marginalization). (2) Label-free distribution or manifold alignment: Degenhart 2020; NoMAD (Karpowicz et al., Nature Communications 2025). All of these start from a supervised-trained decoder and correct drift. None solves cold-start symbol naming.

### Cited Findings
- Jarosiewicz et al. (2015), "Virtual typing by people with tetraplegia using a self-calibrating intracortical brain-computer interface", Science Translational Medicine 7(313): 313ra179. Retrospective target inference (RTI) uses the user's later selections (e.g., typed letters) to infer intended movement targets and retrain the decoder during use. — [Neuroscience News summary](https://neurosciencenews.com/brain-gate-bci-paralysis-3058/); [Einstein Pure record](https://einstein.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/virtual-typing-by-people-with-tetraplegia-using-a-self-calibratin)
- Fan, Hahn, Kamdar, Avansino, Wilson, Hochberg, Shenoy, Henderson & Willett (2023), "Plug-and-Play Stability for Intracortical Brain-Computer Interfaces", NeurIPS 2023 (DOI 10.52202/075280-1831). CORP uses language models to correct errors in iBCI output and uses the corrected outputs as pseudo-labels to update the decoder online. Over 403 days with one participant it reached "a stable decoding accuracy of 93.84% in an online handwriting iBCI task", described as the longest-running human iBCI stability demonstration. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/83a14a36de4502bac5b580db36e81858-Abstract.html)
- Cowan & Linderman (2026), "Online Bayesian Recalibration of Brain–Computer Interfaces with Language Model Potentials", NeurIPS 2026 poster (listed for December 8, 2026). B-CORP "replaces hard pseudo-labels with a soft top-K marginalization", models parameter drift, and recasts pseudo-label recalibration as approximate Bayesian filtering. Reported results: WER "consistently under 3%" on a handwriting BCI dataset, ~30% improvement over the prior state of the art, and robustness when pseudo-labels are unreliable. Tested on synthetic drifting regression and on handwriting and speech BCI data. Abstract-level; I did not read the full paper. — [NeurIPS 2026 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/151000)
- Degenhart, Bishop, Oby, Tyler-Kabara, Chase, Batista & Yu (2020), "Stabilization of a brain-computer interface via the alignment of low-dimensional spaces of neural activity", Nature Biomedical Engineering 4(7):672–685. It aligns low-dimensional manifolds without knowledge of user intent. In monkeys under severe, abrupt recording instabilities it restored proficient online cursor control across days and "can outperform supervised recalibration" (press/record summary). — [CMU RI record](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/stabilization-of-a-brain-computer-interface-via-the-alignment-of-low-dimensional-spaces-of-neural-activity); [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/archive-ni/news/2020/april/stabilizing-brain-computer-interfaces.html)
- Karpowicz et al. (2025), "Stabilizing brain-computer interfaces through alignment of latent dynamics" (NoMAD), Nature Communications 16 (DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-59652-y; bioRxiv 2022.04.06.487388). It combines RNN latent dynamics with unsupervised distribution alignment and gives stable decoding over weeks to months in monkey motor cortex "without any supervised recalibration". — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-025-59652-y.html); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487388.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- CORP and B-CORP are the closest methodological match to our continuous-recalibration stage (c): LM-corrected output as pseudo-labels, with soft marginalization in B-CORP. They assume a decoder that is already roughly right on day 0. Our step (b), choosing among 24 global relabelings before any decoder exists, has no counterpart there. B-CORP's soft top-K marginalization is the natural template for pseudo-labels that also carry permutation uncertainty.
- Degenhart/NoMAD-style alignment is the BCI analog of "align clusters to the previous wear's clusters". With a physically rotated 4-sensor ring, alignment to a reference wear could constrain or even solve the permutation without any language. That makes alignment a necessary baseline to compare the LM approach against.

### Gaps
- I did not verify B-CORP's datasets, number of days, or exact baselines from the full paper.
- I found no 2024–2026 speech-BCI paper that uses LM pseudo-labels from a cold start (no supervised day-0 data). Absence in my search is not proof of absence.

## Q3. Decipherment and side-channel analogs: clusters labeled by language statistics

### Takeaway
This is the strongest prior art for steps (a)+(b)+(c) together. Zhuang, Zhou & Tygar (CCS 2005) cluster unlabeled keystroke sounds and label the clusters with an HMM over English letter statistics plus spelling/grammar correction. They then use the recovered text to train a supervised classifier in a feedback loop, recovering up to 96% of characters from 10 minutes of audio. Classical substitution-cipher decipherment (Knight et al. 2006; Ravi & Knight 2008), lost-language decipherment (Snyder et al. 2010), unsupervised OCR (Ocular 2013) and unsupervised ASR (wav2vec-U 2021) give the algorithmic toolbox.

### Cited Findings
- Zhuang, Zhou & Tygar (2005), "Keyboard Acoustic Emanations Revisited", Proc. 12th ACM CCS, November 2005; an extended version appeared in ACM TISSEC. From a 10-minute recording of English typing it recovers "up to 96% of typed characters" with "no need for training recordings labeled with the corresponding clear text". It combines cepstrum features, HMMs, linear classification and "feedback-based incremental learning", and uses "the statistical constraints of the underlying content, English language". The bootstrapped recognizer then also recognizes random passwords: 90% of 5-character letter passwords in under 20 attempts, 80% of 10-character passwords in under 75. — [Berkeley TISSEC PDF](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/%7Etygar/papers/Keyboard_Acoustic_Emanations_Revisited/TISSEC.pdf); [UT Austin copy](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~shmat/courses/cs6431/zhuang.pdf)
- Berger, Wool & Yeredor (2006), "Dictionary Attacks Using Keyboard Acoustic Emanations", ACM CCS 2006. No training: from a single recording of one word (under 5 s), it reconstructs 7–13-character words using a dictionary. Success is 90% or better for the correct word in the top 50 candidates for words of 10 or more characters. It uses constraints from key similarity rather than cluster identities. — [Wool PDF](https://www.avishaiwool.sites.tau.ac.il/_files/ugd/5dd4a3_2e14e058f2ef4c24ac188a26b1b5c6a4.pdf); [CCS 2006 paper list](https://www.sigsac.org/ccs/CCS2006/paper-list.html)
- Fürst & Aßmuth (2025), "Practical Acoustic Eavesdropping On Typed Passphrases", Cloud Computing 2025 (arXiv:2503.16719). Unsupervised clustering of keystroke sounds (cross-correlation features beat MFCC/FFT) combined with dictionary attacks. Partial recovery speeds up passphrase cracking compared with brute force. No training data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16719)
- Maiti, Jadliwala et al. (2015), "(Smart)watch your taps: side-channel keystroke inference attacks using smartwatches", ISWC 2015. It infers keystrokes from smartwatch motion sensors, but with supervised learning, so it is not an unsupervised analog. A later TMC/WiSec line exists. — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/smart-watch-your-taps-side-channel-keystroke-inference-attacks-using-smartwatches/); [TMC 2018 PDF](https://sprite.utsa.edu/publications/articles/maitiTMC18.pdf)
- MoLe (Wang et al., MobiCom 2015, "MoLe: Motion leaks through smartwatch sensors") infers typed words from smartwatch motion using keyboard geometry and a dictionary. Unverified in this session: I did not retrieve the primary source, so its labeling and training regime is not confirmed here.
- Knight, Nair, Rathod & Yamada (2006), "Unsupervised Analysis for Decipherment Problems", COLING/ACL 2006 Poster Sessions, pp. 499–506. It applies EM-based decipherment to letter substitution ciphers, character-code conversion, phonetic decipherment and word-based ciphers. — [ACL Anthology P06-2065](https://aclanthology.org/P06-2065/)
- Ravi & Knight (2008), "Attacking Decipherment Problems Optimally with Low-Order N-gram Models", EMNLP 2008, pp. 812–819. It solves a substitution cipher exactly as an integer program whose objective is a low-order character LM. — [ACL Anthology D08-1085](https://aclanthology.org/D08-1085/)
- Snyder, Barzilay & Knight (2010), "A Statistical Model for Lost Language Decipherment", ACL 2010. A non-parametric Bayesian model jointly learns alphabet mappings and cognates. On Ugaritic it mapped 29 of 30 letters correctly to Hebrew and found the correct cognate for 60% of words that have one. — [ACL Anthology P10-1107](https://aclanthology.org/P10-1107)
- Berg-Kirkpatrick, Durrett & Klein (2013), "Unsupervised Transcription of Historical Documents" (Ocular), ACL 2013, pp. 207–217. It jointly models a character LM and a glyph-rendering process and learns fonts without supervision. WER drops 31% relative to the leading commercial system and 47% relative to Tesseract. — [ACL Anthology P13-1021](https://aclanthology.org/P13-1021/)
- Baevski, Hsu, Conneau & Auli (2021), "Unsupervised Speech Recognition" (wav2vec-U), NeurIPS 2021. It segments unlabeled audio from self-supervised representations and learns the segment-to-phoneme mapping adversarially against unpaired text. TIMIT PER falls from 26.1 to 11.3 against the previous best unsupervised result, and Librispeech test-other WER is 5.9. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/ea159dc9788ffac311592613b7f71fbb-Abstract.html)

### Inferences
- Our problem is a small, many-to-one homophonic substitution cipher: 4 cipher symbols, each standing for a letter group (T9-like). The plaintext is English letters or words, and the key space is 24. Ravi & Knight's exact search is trivial here: enumerate 24 keys and score each with an LM or dictionary.
- The difficulty is the opposite of Zhuang et al.'s. They have ~30+ clusters but high per-symbol information. We have only 2 bits per stroke and a code that is ambiguous by design. How quickly the permutation becomes identifiable depends on asymmetry in the letter-group frequencies and on which of the 24 decodings produce valid dictionary words. Some permutations may yield many plausible words. This needs a quantitative identifiability analysis (e.g., expected posterior over the 24 keys against number of words), which none of the cited works provides for a 4-symbol code.
- Zhuang et al.'s feedback loop (LM-labeled text, then supervised classifier, then relabel) is essentially our stage (c), demonstrated in 2005 for keyboards.

### Gaps
- I did not extract Zhuang et al.'s per-iteration accuracy (before vs after feedback) from the full PDF; only the abstract-level 96% is cited.
- MoLe's method and numbers are unverified.

## Q4. Unsupervised or self-supervised cross-session/rewear calibration of EMG/IMU gesture recognizers using task or language context

### Takeaway
EMG cross-day and rewear adaptation is well studied with label-free domain adaptation, self-training and feature-statistic alignment (SCADANN 2020; Odeyemi & Zhang 2026). All of it assumes class identities carry over from a labeled source session. None uses language or text statistics to name classes.

### Cited Findings
- Côté-Allard et al. (2020), "Unsupervised Domain Adversarial Self-Calibration for Electromyography-Based Gesture Recognition" (SCADANN), IEEE Access (arXiv:1912.11037). Three steps: DANN on labeled and unlabeled data; pseudo-labeling that uses the prediction's temporal context; retraining with the pseudo-labels plus DANN. Gains: +12±5.2% user-to-user, +9.6±5.0% day-to-day and +8.6±3.3% location-to-location (electrode shift) over no recalibration. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.11037); [IEEE Access/DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/20ea22e42824448c8b160dd934de076b)
- Odeyemi & Zhang (2026), "Recognition and Label-Free Adaptation Across Recording Sessions in Surface-EMG Gesture Decoding", arXiv:2607.27568 (submitted July 30, 2026). It addresses electrode re-attachment with a montage-agnostic encoder on NinaPro DB6 (10 subjects). Cross-session macro-F1 is 0.688 for the encoder vs 0.540 for per-user LDA. Feature-statistic alignment was the only label-free method that helped all subjects and recovers about what one labeled calibration repetition would. It uses no language or cluster-to-label assignment. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27568)
- Related recent label-free EMG adaptation, seen only as search hits with contents unverified: "Lightweight Test-Time Adaptation for EMG-Based Gesture Recognition" (arXiv:2601.04181) and "EMG-UP: Unsupervised Personalization in Cross-User EMG Gesture Recognition" (arXiv:2509.21589). — [arXiv 2601.04181](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04181v2); [arXiv 2509.21589](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21589v1)
- Feasibility of wearable EMG armbands combined with unsupervised transfer learning for seamless myoelectric control (bioRxiv 2022). Search hit only. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.01.06.475232v1)

### Inferences
- In the EMG literature, SCADANN's use of "prediction context" (temporal smoothing within a held gesture) is the nearest thing to task-structure supervision. Language-level context is absent.
- These methods treat electrode shift as a covariate shift with fixed class identity. For a physically rotated sensor ring the shift may instead act like a relabeling, which is our 24-permutation problem. Alignment-based methods would need to handle it explicitly.

### Gaps
- I found no EMG/IMU paper in which a language model or text statistics supply the supervisory signal for rewear recalibration.

## Q5. Has anyone combined (a) per-session unsupervised clustering of a wearable gesture signal, (b) an LM to resolve the cluster-to-symbol mapping, (c) continuous pseudo-label recalibration, for a text-entry wearable?

### Takeaway
I found no work that does all three for a wearable text-entry device. The closest matches each lack one element or differ in setting:
- Zhuang et al. 2005 has a+b+c, but it is an acoustic keyboard attack: an adversary, a non-wearable, ~30+ clusters, one-to-one key mapping.
- Kindermans 2012 / Hübner 2017 have unsupervised training plus an LM plus continuous adaptation, but in EEG, with no symbol permutation; the stimulus design breaks the symmetry.
- CORP 2023 / B-CORP 2026 have b-like LM correction plus c, but for intracortical BCI from a supervised initial decoder, with no clustering or permutation step.
- SCADANN 2020 / Odeyemi & Zhang 2026 have wearable EMG, label-free rewear adaptation and pseudo-labels, but no language.

The remaining gap, as far as I could find, is cold-start naming of a small, ambiguous (many-to-one) wearable gesture alphabet from language after each rewear, followed by LM-pseudo-label continual recalibration.

### Cited Findings
- Acoustic keyboard, a+b+c: [Zhuang, Zhou & Tygar 2005](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/%7Etygar/papers/Keyboard_Acoustic_Emanations_Revisited/TISSEC.pdf)
- Unsupervised clustering plus dictionary, with no explicit LM-based mapping or recalibration loop: [Fürst & Aßmuth 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16719); [Berger, Wool & Yeredor 2006](https://www.avishaiwool.sites.tau.ac.il/_files/ugd/5dd4a3_2e14e058f2ef4c24ac188a26b1b5c6a4.pdf)
- ERP, LM prior plus unsupervised EM: [Kindermans et al. NIPS 2012](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c042f4db68f23406c6cecf84a7ebb0fe-Paper.pdf); [Hübner et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07213)
- iBCI, LM pseudo-labels for continual recalibration: [Fan et al. NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/83a14a36de4502bac5b580db36e81858-Abstract.html); [Cowan & Linderman NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/151000)
- Wearable EMG, label-free rewear adaptation without language: [Côté-Allard et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.11037); [Odeyemi & Zhang 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27568)
- Generic searches for a wearable gesture decoder with language-resolved cluster naming returned no matching paper, only unrelated gesture-clustering or patent hits. — [search hit example: Springer 2025 wearable uncoded gesture system](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40843-025-3553-7)

### Inferences
- Novelty claims should be narrow. "Calibration-free via language" is established in ERP spellers (2012–2018) and keyboard side channels (2005). "LM pseudo-labels for continual recalibration" is established in iBCIs (2023–2026). What appears new is (i) the wearable rewear setting, (ii) a global discrete permutation latent over a 4-symbol, many-to-one code, and (iii) chaining permutation identification into continual recalibration.
- Required baselines that reviewers will expect:
  - Alignment to a previous wear without language (Degenhart/NoMAD or feature-statistic alignment). For a rotated ring, physical geometry may restrict plausible relabelings to cyclic shifts or reflections (at most 8 dihedral candidates instead of 24). This is a hypothesis only: the project rules note that cyclic reindexing is not equivalent to physically moving sensors, so it must be checked against real rewear data.
  - EM with an LM prior (Kindermans).
  - Soft vs hard pseudo-labels (B-CORP).
  - Matched shuffled-label or random-permutation controls (project rule).
- Key quantity to report: identifiability of the true permutation against number of words or strokes, given ~93% cluster purity and the T9-like grouping. The ERP literature shows the LM mainly shortens warm-up, so time-to-correct-permutation is the meaningful metric.

### Gaps
- Coverage of IMWUT/CHI/UIST for wearable text entry with self-calibration was limited by my search budget. A targeted IMWUT search could still turn up a closer match (e.g., ring/IMU typing with unsupervised adaptation).
- I did not check whether any keystroke-inference paper uses a T9-style many-to-one grouping with cluster naming.
- I did not verify MoLe (MobiCom 2015) or the 2014 Kindermans online zero-training study at the primary source.

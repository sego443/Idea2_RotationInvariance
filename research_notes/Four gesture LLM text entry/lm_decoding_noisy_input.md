# Language-model decoding of noisy, uncertain or low-bandwidth input for text entry (Bayesian/noisy-channel through LLMs, 2023-2026)

Scope note: notes compiled 2026-10-17 from web search and fetches (about 16 tool calls). Items marked UNVERIFIED come from the researcher's background knowledge and were not confirmed against a primary source in this session; the report writer should treat them as leads, not facts. All "reported" numbers below are as stated by the cited source; "Inferences" are this researcher's reasoning.

Project context used for relevance: 4-channel piezoelectric wristband, 4 directional swipes, ~96% per-stroke accuracy (project figure, presegmented, same-session; not re-verified here), each letter mapped to one of 4 symbols (T9-like ambiguous code), goal is an n-gram/LLM decoder consuming soft per-stroke posteriors.

## Q1. BCI text decoding with LMs: how much did LMs reduce error, and how were soft decoder outputs combined?

### Takeaway
Every high-performance intracortical text BCI since 2021 uses the same pattern: a neural network emits per-frame character or phoneme probabilities (CTC-style), an n-gram WFST/beam search generates hypotheses with a large vocabulary, and (since 2023) a transformer LLM rescores the n-best list; in the 2024 benchmark the biggest gains came from merging several decoders' outputs with a fine-tuned LLM (9.7% -> 5.8% WER). Language modelling routinely turns a raw signal that is only "OK" (roughly 6% character error, about 25% word error) into usable text.

### Cited Findings
- Willett et al., "High-performance brain-to-text communication via handwriting", Nature 593:249-254 (2021): attempted-handwriting intracortical BCI, 90 characters/min, 94.1% raw online accuracy, ">99% accuracy offline with a general-purpose autocorrect" — [NIH summary](https://www.nih.gov/node/237346); [Stanford Neuroscience](https://neuroscience.stanford.edu/node/1538); [PMC8163299](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8163299/)
- Same paper: a character error rate of 0.89% is quoted for the LM-corrected condition — [Fermat's Library annotation](https://fermatslibrary.com/s/high-performance-brain-to-text-communication-via-handwriting). UNVERIFIED detail: online raw CER about 5.4% and WER about 25% in copy typing; offline beam search with a large-vocabulary n-gram LM giving 0.89% CER and about 3.4% WER; free typing about 74 CPM. The primary PDF could not be fetched (socket errors / Nature paywall redirect) to confirm.
- Willett et al., "A high-performance speech neuroprosthesis", Nature 620 (2023): 62 WPM; 9.1% WER on a 50-word vocabulary and 23.8% WER on a 125,000-word vocabulary — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v620y2023i7976d10.1038_s41586-023-06377-x.html); [bioRxiv preprint](https://biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.21.524489v1). UNVERIFIED detail: an RNN outputs phoneme probabilities, decoded with a Kaldi-style n-gram WFST (125k words); an offline improved LM (larger n-gram plus OPT transformer rescoring) lowered WER further (about 11-12% is the figure remembered, not confirmed).
- Metzger et al., Nature 620 (2023), ECoG speech + avatar: text decoding at a median 78 WPM with a median 25% WER on a 1024-word vocabulary — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v620y2023i7976d10.1038_s41586-023-06443-4.html); [UCSF Engineering](https://engineering.ucsf.edu/publications/high-performance-neuroprosthesis-speech-decoding-and-avatar-control). UNVERIFIED: CTC phone/character outputs decoded with beam search plus an n-gram LM restricted to the 1024-word vocabulary.
- Card et al., "An Accurate and Rapidly Calibrating Speech Neuroprosthesis", NEJM 391:609-618 (Aug 2024): 256 electrodes in ventral precentral gyrus (ALS participant); 99.6% word accuracy with a 50-word vocabulary on day 1 (25 days post-implant); 90.2% on day 2 with a 125,000-word vocabulary after 1.4 h more training; 97.5% accuracy sustained over 8.4 months; about 32 WPM in self-paced conversation over >248 h — [PubMed 39141853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39141853/); [PMC11328962](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11328962/); [Dryad data](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.dncjsxm85). UNVERIFIED: the pipeline is RNN phoneme logits -> 5-gram WFST -> OPT-6.7B rescoring, like the benchmark baseline below.
- Brain-to-Text Benchmark '24 (Willett et al., arXiv 2412.17227, Dec 2024): held-out private set of 1200 sentences scored by WER; the baseline is an RNN trained with CTC to produce phoneme logits, then a 5-gram hypothesis-generation stage, then LLM rescoring; baseline 9.7% WER, top entrant 5.8% WER. "The largest improvements in accuracy were achieved using an ensembling approach, where the output of multiple independent decoders was merged using a fine-tuned large language model", which all three top entrants used. Better RNN training (learning-rate schedule, diphone objective) also helped; deep state-space models and transformers did not yet beat the RNN — [arXiv 2412.17227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17227v1); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17227v1)
- Non-invasive analogue: Meta Reality Labs (Kaifosh, Reardon et al.), "A generic non-invasive neuromotor interface for human-computer interaction", Nature 645:702-711 (2025). An sEMG wristband gives cross-user handwriting at 20.9 WPM (closed-loop median); personalising the model improves handwriting decoding by 16% — [CSHL repository](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/42049/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v645y2025i8081d10.1038_s41586-025-09255-w.html); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.02.23.581779v2). Whether and how an LM was used in the handwriting CER pipeline was not confirmed in this session (gap).

### Inferences
- The decoder architecture the project needs already exists in the BCI work: per-frame or per-stroke soft posteriors -> lexicon/n-gram WFST beam search -> LLM n-best rescoring (optionally an LLM that merges several hypotheses). Swapping "phoneme posteriors" for "per-stroke 4-symbol posteriors" is mechanically straightforward; what changes is how ambiguous the code is.
- In BCIs the LM gain is large because raw WER is high (about 25%) while CER is moderate. A 96%-per-stroke, 4-symbol code is far more ambiguous per character than a 26-way character posterior, so the LM carries much more of the load. Gains will depend on vocabulary and domain far more than in the BCI case.
- The wristband sEMG Nature paper is the closest wearable-wristband precedent for text entry, but it uses full handwriting (26+ classes), not a 4-symbol code.

### Gaps
- Exact Willett 2021 raw vs LM CER/WER and Willett 2023 offline-LM WER were not confirmed from primary text.
- Brain-to-Text '25 (Card et al. dataset Kaggle competition) results and 2025-2026 LLM-rescoring papers (for example LISA / Benster et al. 2024, "LLM error correction for BCI") were not retrieved; their existence and numbers are UNVERIFIED.

## Q2. P300/ERP, SSVEP, RSVP spellers and Dasher with language models

### Takeaway
Noninvasive ERP spellers were the first BCIs to fuse classifier evidence with an n-gram LM via Bayesian updating (posterior proportional to EEG likelihood times LM prior), recursively across repeated flashes. This is exactly the "soft posterior x LM prior" recipe the project needs, and it reports 40-75% bit-rate gains.

### Cited Findings
- Speier et al. (UCLA): adding natural-language processing to the P300 speller significantly improved accuracy and raised bit rate by 40-60% for all six subjects in a pilot study — [UCLA Smart Health](https://smarthealth.ucla.edu/publication/natural-language-processing-with-dynamic-classification-improves-p300-speller-accuracy-and-bit-rate/); [PMC3360927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3360927)
- Speier et al., ECoG P300: accuracy 82.77% and bit rate 41.02 rose to 96.31% and 49.47 with an LM plus spectral features — [Speier, academia.edu](https://ucla.academia.edu/WilliamSpeier)
- Speier: particle-filter incorporation of language priors, Baum-Welch unsupervised training, Gibbs-sampled electrode selection; an online system with only four electrodes gave an average bit rate 75% above standard methods — [IEEE SPS newsletter 2015](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/newsletter/2015/05/speier-william-farran-iv-university-california-los-angeles-toward-real-time)
- RSVP Keyboard (Orhan, Erdogmus, Oken et al., OHSU/Northeastern): Bayesian fusion of an n-gram symbol LM with regularized-discriminant-analysis EEG ERP features; the LM "contribute[s] significantly to letter classification accuracy"; recursive Bayesian estimation with non-committing string decisions beat one-shot fusion; the LM lets each target be shown only 1-4 times — [Orhan et al., "Improved accuracy using recursive Bayesian estimation based language model fusion"](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/improved-accuracy-using-recursive-bayesian-estimation-based-langu/); [PMC3771530](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3771530); [PMC4065780](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4065780)
- "Towards Predictive Communication with Brain-Computer Interfaces integrating Large Language Models" (2024 position/review) exists — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386963732_Towards_Predictive_Communication_with_Brain-Computer_Interfaces_integrating_Large_Language_Models); contents not reviewed.
- UNVERIFIED (not fetched): Dasher (Ward, Blackwell, MacKay, UIST 2000) allocates screen space in proportion to LM probability, and Wills & MacKay (IEEE TNSRE 2006) "DASHER — an efficient writing system for brain-computer interfaces?" studied it with BCI-like control. SSVEP spellers with LM-driven predictive layouts also exist; no citation was confirmed.

### Inferences
- RSVP Keyboard's recursive Bayesian fusion, with no commitment until confidence is high, is the closest classical analogue to "keep the soft 4-way posterior for each stroke and let the LM decide at word/sentence end".
- Dasher's arithmetic-coding view shows that a low-bandwidth channel can be information-efficient when the LM shapes the input. A 4-symbol channel at 96% accuracy carries at most about 1.75 bits/stroke (estimate: log2(4) minus the confusion entropy, assuming errors are spread uniformly), compared with roughly 1-1.5 bits/char of English entropy under strong LMs.

### Gaps
- Quantitative RSVP Keyboard accuracy/ITR numbers and SSVEP+LM numbers were not retrieved.

## Q3. Bayesian/noisy-channel decoding of touch and gesture typing (classical HCI)

### Takeaway
The noisy-channel formulation P(text | signal) proportional to P(signal | text) x P_LM(text) was established for soft keyboards in 2002 (error reduced by a factor of 1.67-1.87) and scaled to sentence-level decoding (VelociTap: 41 WPM at 3% CER on a 40 mm keyboard). These decoders are the template for a stroke-posterior x LM decoder.

### Cited Findings
- Goodman, Venolia, Steury, Parker, "Language modeling for soft keyboards", IUI 2002 / AAAI 2002: combining a key-press (touch-location) model with a character LM; error rate reduced overall by a factor of 1.67-1.87 — [MSR PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/p194-goodman.pdf); [ACM DL 10.1145/502716.502753](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/502716.502753); [MLAnthology AAAI 2002](https://mlanthology.org/aaai/2002/goodman2002aaai-language)
- Vertanen, Memmi, Emge, Reyal, Kristensson, "VelociTap: Investigating fast mobile text entry using sentence-based decoding of touchscreen keyboard input", CHI 2015: novices reach a mean 41 WPM at 3% CER on a 40 mm-wide smartwatch-sized keyboard; spaces can be typed, swiped or omitted and inferred by the decoder — [PDF](https://pokristensson.com/pubs/VertanenEtAlCHI2015.pdf); [keithv.com](https://www.keithv.com/pub/velocitap); [St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/6884)
- Related: VelociWatch (Vertanen et al.) — [PDF](https://www.keithv.com/pub/velociwatch/vertanen-velociwatch.pdf); "Effects of Language Modeling and its Personalization on Touchscreen Typing Performance" (Fowler et al., CHI 2015, Google) — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2702123.2702503) (numbers not extracted). UNVERIFIED memory: Fowler et al. report that removing the LM raises WER from about 4% to more than 20%.
- Vertanen & Kristensson et al., "A Dataset of Noisy Typing on QWERTY Keyboards", IUI 2023 — noted in [Keith Vertanen publications](https://www.keithv.com/pub/).
- UNVERIFIED (not fetched this session): Kristensson & Zhai SHARK2 (UIST 2004) template-matching gesture-keyboard decoder with LM integration; Weir, Pohl, Rogers, Vertanen, Kristensson "Uncertain text entry on mobile devices" (CHI 2014; touch-pressure-modulated uncertainty) — title appears in [AMiner](https://www.aminer.org/pub/53e9b879b7602d97044454f2/uncertain-text-entry-on-mobile-devices); Schwarz, Hudson, Mankoff, Wilson "A framework for robust and flexible handling of inputs with uncertainty" (UIST 2010); Dudley, Vertanen, Kristensson head-mounted-display decoders.

### Inferences
- A per-stroke 4-way posterior plugs directly into the "key-press model" slot of Goodman/VelociTap: P(signal | letter) = sum over symbols of P(stroke posterior | symbol) x 1[letter maps to symbol]. VelociTap-style token-passing beam search over a character n-gram with word-boundary inference is the obvious baseline decoder.
- Goodman's factor of 1.7-1.9 came from mildly noisy input on a 26-key layout. With a many-to-one 4-symbol map the LM's share of the work is much larger, so classical gains do not transfer numerically.

### Gaps
- SHARK2, Weir 2014 and Schwarz 2010 numbers were not verified.

## Q4. LLMs for text entry 2023-2026 (AAC, abbreviation expansion, LLM keyboard decoders, minimal-key keyboards)

### Takeaway
LLMs are now used (a) to expand heavily abbreviated input (SpeakFaster: 57% more motor actions saved than predictive keyboards, 29-60% faster for ALS eye-gaze users), (b) as end-to-end keyboard decoders from serialized noisy taps/gestures (CHI 2025 FLAN-T5: 93.1% top-1 on gestures, 95.4% on taps), and (c) to disambiguate extreme ambiguous keyboards. A June 2026 arXiv study reports 9.5% CER with 3 keys and 5.4% with 5 keys, from hard key sequences and GPT-4o selection.

### Cited Findings
- Cai et al., "Using large language models to accelerate communication for eye gaze typing users with ALS", Nature Communications (Nov 2024) (SpeakFaster; arXiv 2312.01532): fine-tuned LLMs plus conversational context expand word-initial abbreviations into full phrases; 57% more motor actions saved than traditional predictive keyboards in offline simulation; text-entry rates 29-60% above baselines for two eye-gaze ALS users; a 19-participant mobile pilot matched the simulated motor savings with small changes in typing speed — [PMC11530652](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11530652); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/speakfaster-revolutionizing-communication-for-people-with-severe-motor-impairments/); [arXiv 2312.01532](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.01532v1)
- Cai et al., "Context-Aware Abbreviation Expansion Using Large Language Models" (NAACL 2022; arXiv 2205.03767) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.03767); and "Parameter Efficient Tuning Allows Scalable Personalization of LLMs for Text Entry: A Case Study on Abbreviation Expansion" (arXiv 2312.14327) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14327v1). Numbers not extracted.
- Ma, Zhang, Ramakrishnan, Bi, "LLM Powered Text Entry Decoding and Flexible Typing on Smartphones", CHI 2025 (DOI 10.1145/3706598.3714314): a fine-tuned FLAN-T5 decoder gives 93.1% top-1 on user-drawn gestures (beating SHARK2) and 95.4% on real-world tap typing; in the user study words were entered 35.9% by gesture, 29.0% by taps, 6.1% by multi-stroke gestures and 29.0% by tap-gestures — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3706598.3714314); [Stony Brook](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/llm-powered-text-entry-decoding-and-flexible-typing-on-smartphone/)
- "KeySense: LLM-Powered Hands-Down, Ten-Finger Typing on Commodity Touchscreens" (arXiv 2602.12432, Feb 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12432v1); numbers not extracted.
- Gaines & Vertanen, "Adapting Large Language Models for Character-based Augmentative and Alternative Communication" (arXiv 2501.10582; reported as EMNLP 2025): an algorithm that turns a subword LLM into character predictions is more accurate than a classification layer, a byte-level LLM, or an n-gram model on AAC-like text; studies in-domain adaptation with small sets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10582)
- Kimura (LY Corporation), "3-Key-Input: Exploring the Theoretical Minimum Keys for Text Entry", arXiv 2606.11642 (10 Jun 2026): 2-5 physical keys; layout-, frequency- and adversarial letter-to-key maps; decoders are trie-only, GPT-2 beam search and GPT-4o candidate selection (temperature 0.2). GPT-4o results: 2 keys 23.3% CER / 33.7% WER; 3 keys 9.5% / 12.2%; 5 keys 5.4% / 6.8%; key-stream entropy 0.97 / 1.54 / 2.26 bits/char. Mapping choice changes CER by <0.5 pp. Domain CER: business 9.5%, conversational 11.2%, technical 20.1%. Input is hard (error-free) key sequences, there is no noise model, evaluation is offline sentence-level on a 300-sentence proprietary corpus, and there is no user study or WPM — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11642); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11642). (The fetched summary did not give a separate 4-key row; check the PDF.)
- UNVERIFIED: KWickChat (Shen, Yang, Kristensson, IUI 2022), a GPT-2-based keyword-to-sentence AAC system, and other 2023-2025 LLM-AAC work (for example Valencia et al. CHI 2023 on LLM phrase generation for AAC users) were not fetched.

### Inferences
- The 3-Key-Input paper is the closest published match to the project's 4-symbol code. It brackets expected performance at 4 keys with perfect input somewhere between 9.5% and 5.4% CER (interpolated estimate, not reported), and shows the letter-to-symbol assignment matters little (<0.5 pp) for reasonable maps. Adding 4% per-stroke noise (each letter is one stroke, so about 4% symbol errors per letter) will raise this unless the decoder uses soft posteriors. That paper's trie enumeration assumes error-free keys.
- The CHI 2025 FLAN-T5 work shows a seq2seq LLM can learn the noisy-input-to-word mapping directly when serialized noisy signals are used for training. The analogous step would be fine-tuning on synthetic 4-symbol sequences with simulated confusions.

### Gaps
- SpeakFaster per-condition WPM and abbreviation-expansion top-5 accuracy, KWickChat keystroke savings, and the 4-key row of 3-Key-Input were not extracted.

## Q5. Any work decoding text from a 4-symbol (or similarly tiny) noisy alphabet with an LLM using soft posteriors?

### Takeaway
No published work was found that does exactly this: an LLM decoding a 4-symbol (or 2-5 symbol) ambiguous letter code from soft, noisy per-symbol posteriors. The nearest neighbours are each missing one ingredient: 3-Key-Input (LLM + tiny alphabet, but hard error-free input); BCI CTC+n-gram+LLM pipelines (soft posteriors + LLM, but a large unambiguous phoneme/character alphabet); RSVP/P300 Bayesian fusion (soft posteriors + n-gram, tiny selection steps, but no many-to-one letter code and no LLM).

### Cited Findings
- 3-Key-Input: tiny alphabet, LLM decoding, hard input, no noise model — [arXiv 2606.11642](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11642)
- Brain-to-Text '24: soft phoneme logits -> 5-gram -> LLM rescoring and LLM ensembling — [arXiv 2412.17227](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17227v1)
- RSVP Keyboard: soft EEG evidence x n-gram prior, recursive Bayesian — [Orhan et al.](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/improved-accuracy-using-recursive-bayesian-estimation-based-langu/)
- Goodman 2002 and VelociTap: soft touch likelihood x LM, but 26+ keys — [Goodman PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/p194-goodman.pdf); [VelociTap PDF](https://pokristensson.com/pubs/VertanenEtAlCHI2015.pdf)
- Adjacent signal-processing analogue: "LLM-Viterbi: Semantic-Aware Decoding for Convolutional Codes" (arXiv 2604.19035) uses an LM to score the linguistic plausibility of character sequences inside a channel decoder — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19035) (not read in detail).

### Inferences
- The novelty claim "soft-posterior LLM decoding of a 4-symbol noisy wristband code" looks defensible against this search, but the search was not exhaustive (see Gaps). Classical ambiguous-keyboard literature (T9, LetterWise, 4-key / one-key ambiguous keyboards, eye-gesture 4-direction typing) should be checked before claiming novelty for the code itself; the novelty is more likely in the soft-input LLM decoding plus the physical sensor.
- A recommended experiment, by analogy with the BCI literature (a design proposal, not a result): compare hard argmax symbols + LLM against soft posteriors + n-gram beam + LLM rescoring, matched against a shuffled-posterior control, and report CER/WER against vocabulary size.

### Gaps
- Not searched: classical 4-key ambiguous keyboards (e.g., Lesher et al. 1998 optimal ambiguous layouts; MacKenzie's LetterWise; Tanaka-Ishii; 4-direction eye-gesture or EdgeWrite-like ambiguous codes; "one-key"/"two-key" keyboards for switch users), and wearable gesture-to-ambiguous-code text entry (smartwatch/ring 4-direction swipes). These could hold close precedents with n-gram decoding.

## Q6. Practical decoding methods: constrained beam search, token-to-character mapping, character LMs, open vs closed vocabulary, pitfalls

### Takeaway
Use a character-level (or token-to-character-marginalized) LM so that per-stroke posteriors can be multiplied in at character granularity. Recent methods (ICML 2025, ICLR 2025, EMNLP 2025) give exact or approximate character distributions from subword LLMs, removing "tokenization bias". The practical BCI recipe is an n-gram first pass plus LLM n-best rescoring. Closed vocabularies inflate accuracy, and open-vocabulary claims require unseen-word evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- Vieira, LeBrun, Giulianelli, Gastaldi, DuSell, Terilla, O'Donnell, Cotterell, "From Language Models over Tokens to Language Models over Characters", ICML 2025 (PMLR 267): exact and approximate algorithms convert token-level LMs into character-level LMs; they approximate the character distribution accurately at reasonable speed across four public LMs — [arXiv 2412.03719](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.03719); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/vieira25a.html)
- Phan, Amos, Gat, Havasi, Muckley, Ullrich, "Exact Byte-Level Probabilities from Tokenized Language Models for FIM-Tasks and Model Ensembles", ICLR 2025: identifies "tokenization bias" (next-byte distributions differ even for statistically equivalent tokenized and byte models) and gives a zero-shot next-byte sampling algorithm that removes it — [arXiv 2410.09303](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09303); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/5ed5c3c846f684a54975ad7a2525199f-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Gaines & Vertanen (2025): subword-to-character prediction beats byte-level LLMs and n-grams for AAC character prediction — [arXiv 2501.10582](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10582)
- BCI practical recipe: an n-gram stage for hypothesis generation, then LLM rescoring; LLM-based merging of multiple decoders gave the largest benchmark gains — [arXiv 2412.17227](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17227v1)
- Vocabulary pitfall, illustrated by reported numbers: WER 9.1% (50 words) vs 23.8% (125k words) for the same speech BCI — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v620y2023i7976d10.1038_s41586-023-06377-x.html); 99.6% (50 words) vs 90.2% (125k words, day 2) in Card 2024 — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39141853/)
- Domain pitfall: GPT-4o ambiguous-key decoding CER rises from 9.5% (business) to 20.1% (technical text: specialised lexicons, acronyms) — [arXiv 2606.11642](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11642)

### Inferences
- Recommended decoder stack for the project (design proposal): (1) per-stroke 4-way posteriors, calibrated (temperature-scaled on training data only); (2) a beam search over characters where each letter's score is log P(symbol posterior | letter's symbol) + lambda log P_charLM, with a lexicon trie for closed-vocabulary mode and a pure character LM for open-vocabulary mode; (3) n-best rescoring with an LLM through token-to-character marginalization (Vieira et al. / Phan et al.) or plain whole-sentence LLM scoring of complete candidates (simpler, since each candidate is a full string); (4) optional LLM selection among candidates (3-Key-Input style).
- Pitfalls to flag: closed-vocabulary or fixed-phrase-set evaluation overstating open-vocabulary ability (as the project rules also require); prompt sentences overlapping LM training text (memorization inflates accuracy); LLM "hallucinated fluency", where outputs are plausible but wrong and errors are semantic rather than character-level (inference consistent with LLM-merge approaches, not directly measured here); over-confident (uncalibrated) posteriors that override the LM; LM weight and insertion penalty tuned on the test set; tokenization bias when scoring partial words with subword LLMs.
- Word segmentation is a separate problem: VelociTap shows spaces can be inferred, but with a 4-symbol code without a dedicated space gesture, segmentation ambiguity compounds letter ambiguity. A space/end-of-word gesture or presegmented words, as in the project's current protocol, is advisable.

### Gaps
- No quantitative comparison was found of soft-posterior vs hard-symbol decoding for ambiguous codes. This is an open experimental question for the project.
- LLM rescoring latency and real-time feasibility numbers for BCI pipelines were not retrieved.

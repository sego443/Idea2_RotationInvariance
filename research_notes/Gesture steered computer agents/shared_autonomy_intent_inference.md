# Shared autonomy, intent inference and information-efficient selection interfaces

Scope: theory and methods for combining a noisy, low-bandwidth user signal with an autonomous system's prior to reach the user's goal, judged against our design: a 4-gesture wristband (~96% per-stroke accuracy, ~2 bits/stroke nominal) steering an LLM computer-use agent that proposes 4 candidate actions per step, with the 4 options chosen to minimize expected strokes under gesture noise, plus error recovery and risk-aware confirmation.

Verification legend: items with a [Source](URL) were checked this session against the primary page, the publisher page or an abstract or search snippet. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from background knowledge and were not checked against a primary source this session. Numbers are labelled as "reported" (the authors' figures) or "estimate" (our own arithmetic).

Rough channel capacity (estimate, our arithmetic, not from any source): a 4-ary symmetric channel with 4% symbol error has capacity log2(4) − H(0.96, 0.04/3 ×3) ≈ 2 − (0.2423 + 0.04·log2(75)) ≈ 2 − 0.49 ≈ 1.51 bits/stroke, not 2. Any "bits per stroke" claim for our system should use roughly 1.5 bits as the noisy-channel ceiling, unless errors are detected and erased (an erasure channel gives a higher effective rate).

## Shared autonomy / shared control (policy blending, POMDP hindsight optimization, deep RL, BCI arms and wheelchairs, LLM-era goal inference)

### Takeaway
The classic line works as follows: infer a distribution over goals from noisy input, then either blend toward the most likely goal (Dragan & Srinivasa) or act to maximize expected value under the whole goal belief (Javdani hindsight optimization). Deep RL (Reddy et al.) drops the explicit goal model. All report faster or more successful task completion than unassisted control, but users split on preference because of the trade between control authority and speed. In this literature the user signal is a continuous control input (joystick or decoded velocity). None of it designs a discrete option menu that the user picks from, which is our design.

### Cited Findings
- Dragan & Srinivasa, "A Policy Blending Formalism for Shared Control," IJRR 32(7):790–805, 2013. They cast assistance as policy blending with two parts, intent prediction and arbitration. Their user study indicates that arbitration should be contextual and depend on the robot's confidence in itself and in the user. — [CMU RI](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/a-policy-blending-formalism-for-shared-control); [PDF](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/2013/5/assistive_teleop_IJRR.pdf)
- Earlier conference version: "Formalizing Assistive Teleoperation," RSS 2012. — [RSS VIII](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p10.html)
- Javdani, Srinivasa & Bagnell, "Shared Autonomy via Hindsight Optimization," RSS 2015. Extended version with Admoni, Pellegrinelli & Bagnell in IJRR 2018. They model the problem as a POMDP with uncertainty over the user's goal and approximate it with hindsight optimization (QMDP-like). The robot can assist toward several goals at once without committing to one. — [arXiv 1503.07619](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.07619); [RSS XI](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss11/p32.html); [IJRR PDF](https://personalrobotics.cs.washington.edu/publications/javdani2018shared.pdf)
- Reported (IJRR 2018) for teleoperated grasping: the method reaches goals faster, needs less user input, reduces user idle time and causes fewer collisions than predict-then-act blending. User ratings were mixed, and participants cited a trade-off between control authority and task speed. 10 participants (9 male), all with robotics experience. — [arXiv 1706.00155](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/1706.00155)
- Reddy, Dragan & Levine, "Shared Autonomy via Deep Reinforcement Learning," RSS 2018. Model-free human-in-the-loop deep Q-learning maps observation and user input to action values. The copilot picks the action closest to the user's input among actions whose Q-value is within a tolerance α of the best (α = 0.6 in the user study). Reported: 12 participants in Lunar Lander. Human plus copilot had significantly more successful landings (F(1,11)=165.0, p<0.0001) and fewer crashes (F(1,11)=260.0, p<0.0001) than solo humans. The exact rates were not extracted in this session. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.01744); [RSS XIV](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p05.html); [BAIR blog](https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/2018/04/18/shared-autonomy)
- Follow-up on optimizing intervention frequency in shared autonomy: "On Optimizing Interventions in Shared Autonomy" (Tan et al., AAAI 2022). — [arXiv 2112.09169](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09169)
- Muelling et al., "Autonomy Infused Teleoperation with Application to BCI Manipulation," Autonomous Robots 41(6):1401–1422, 2017. Two tetraplegic participants with intracortical microelectrode BCIs (2.5 years and 2 months post-implant) controlled a 7-DoF Barrett WAM arm with a BarrettHand. Reported: significant performance gains on adapted rehabilitation benchmarks. Shared assistance reduced perceived difficulty and made previously infeasible tasks possible (door opening, pouring, novel objects in clutter). — [arXiv 1503.05451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.05451); [CMU RI](https://ri.cmu.edu/publications/autonomy-infused-teleoperation-with-application-to-bci-manipulation-2)
- Downey et al., "Blending of brain-machine interface and vision-guided autonomous robotics improves neuroprosthetic arm performance during grasping," J. NeuroEng. Rehabil. 2016. Two participants with tetraplegia. A vision system defined grasp poses, and BMI velocity was blended with autonomous grasping near objects. Reported: both participants were more successful on object-transfer tasks with shared control, and movements were more accurate, more efficient and less difficult. The per-subject success percentages were not extracted (the PMC fetch failed). — [PMC4797113](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4797113/)
- Belsare, Karimi, Mattson & Brown, "Toward Zero-Shot User Intent Recognition in Shared Autonomy" (VOSA), HRI 2025. It infers intent from end-effector vision without a predefined goal set and uses blended control. Reported: performance matches an oracle baseline with privileged goal knowledge, effort is significantly lower than unassisted teleoperation, and most participants preferred it across three tabletop tasks. The abstract does not describe LLM or VLM use. — [arXiv 2501.08389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08389)
- BALI (HRI 2026, Yale ScazLab), "Open-Ended Goal Inference through Actions and Language for Human-Robot Collaboration." An LLM generates and keeps updating a set of plausible goals, each scored by alignment with the action history and with language preferences. The robot asks clarifying questions when expected information gain outweighs the interruption cost. This is the closest robotics analog to "LLM prior plus noisy user evidence plus an EIG-based query decision." Quantitative gains were not extracted. — [arXiv 2512.04453](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04453); [HRI 2026 PDF](https://scazlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/HRI_2026___BALI.pdf)
- Other 2025–2026 LLM goal-inference HRI work surfaced but was not read: "Enhancing Goal Inference via Correction Timing" (arXiv 2602.18603), "I've Changed My Mind: Robots Adapting to Changing Human Goals" (arXiv 2511.15914), STEP multimodal-LLM task estimation (arXiv 2608.27225). — [2602.18603](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18603); [2511.15914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15914); [2608.27225](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27225)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** BCI wheelchair shared control (e.g., Millán group, Carlson & Millán 2013, IEEE RAM; Philips et al. 2007) reports that shared control lets novice EEG users navigate with fewer commands and collisions. Not checked this session.

### Inferences
- The Dragan/Javdani split maps directly onto our design. "Blend toward the argmax goal" is like showing the single top-1 action plus three alternatives. "Hindsight optimization over the goal belief" is like choosing the 4 options to maximize expected value over the LLM's whole intent distribution. The Javdani result (acting on the full belief beats committing to one goal) argues for set-level option design over plain top-4 ranking.
- The repeated finding that users trade control authority against speed implies that a fully automatic "agent commits when confident" mode needs an opt-out, and that user preference should be measured, not only stroke counts.
- In BCI shared control, the signal is continuous velocity and the autonomy fills in fine motor detail. Our setting is discrete decision steps over a combinatorial action space, so the gains in this literature do not transfer numerically.

### Gaps
- Exact success-rate numbers for Reddy et al. (Lunar Lander) and Downey et al. (per-subject transfer success) were not extracted.
- I found no shared-autonomy paper where the user's channel is a small discrete alphabet choosing among k autonomy-proposed options for a general computer-use agent.
- Quantitative results for BALI and the other 2025–2026 LLM goal-inference papers were not extracted.

## Information-theoretic / Bayesian selection interfaces (Dasher, BIG/BIGnav, posterior matching, noisy 20 questions, Huffman scanning)

### Takeaway
There is a mature theory for "noisy low-rate channel plus strong prior." It includes arithmetic-coding interfaces (Dasher), Bayesian experimental design that maximizes the mutual information of the next input (BIGnav and BIG), capacity-achieving feedback codes (posterior matching for BCIs) and noisy 20 questions, where greedy one-step entropy reduction is provably optimal for entropy loss. Our "choose 4 options to minimize expected strokes" is a k=4 instance of this family. The new elements are the LLM-over-actions prior and a non-stationary, structured hypothesis space (agent actions), which replaces a fixed alphabet or 1D/2D target space.

### Cited Findings
- Ward & MacKay, "Fast hands-free writing by gaze direction," Nature 418:838, 2002. Dasher is text entry based on inverse arithmetic coding with a language model. Reported: after about one hour of practice, users wrote up to 25 wpm by gaze versus 15 wpm with an on-screen keyboard, and the on-screen keyboard's error rate was about 5 times Dasher's. — [arXiv cs/0204030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0204030); [Dasher press page](https://dasher.acecentre.net/docs/research/press)
- Liu, Lucas d'Oliveira, Beaudouin-Lafon & Rioul, "BIGnav: Bayesian Information Gain for Guiding Multiscale Navigation," CHI 2017. The system keeps a posterior over the intended target, interprets each user command as evidence, and moves to the view that maximizes the expected information gain of the user's next input. Reported: significantly faster than conventional pan-and-zoom, with fewer commands for distant targets, especially in non-uniform information spaces. On a realistic map, likely POIs were reached in a few steps. Exact time and command reductions were not extracted (the PDF link returned 404). — [IP Paris record](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/bignav-bayesian-information-gain-for-guiding-multiscale-navigatio/)
- Liu (Wanyu) thesis/collection "Bayesian Information Gain to Design Interaction" generalizes BIG as a framework for interaction design. — [HAL hal-03323514](https://hal.telecom-paris.fr/hal-03323514)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Liu et al., "BIGFile: Bayesian Information Gain for Fast File Retrieval," CHI 2018, applies BIG to hierarchical file navigation, with reported speed-ups over a standard file browser. Not checked this session.
- Omar et al., "A feedback information-theoretic approach to the design of brain-computer interfaces," Int. J. Human-Computer Interaction, 2011. Tantiongloc et al., "An information and control framework for optimizing user-compliant human computer interfaces," Proc. IEEE, Feb 2017. Both cast the BCI as a cooperative feedback-communication system and use posterior matching schemes (capacity-achieving feedback codes) as the interaction algorithm. — [Stanford Coleman lab BMI page](https://colemanlab.stanford.edu/research/brain-machine-interface); [Coleman neuro-info-theory PDF](https://soihub.org/site/assets/files/3151/colemanneuroinfotheory.pdf)
- Posterior matching theory: Shayevitz & Feder, "Optimal Feedback Communication via Posterior Matching." Optimal-transport generalization to arbitrary dimensions: arXiv 1901.02523. — [arXiv 0909.4828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.4828); [arXiv 1901.02523](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.02523)
- Posterior matching is described as a capacity-achieving code that reaches a given error rate with as few binary inputs as possible. It has been used in non-invasive BCIs for text entry and vehicle path planning, and in a low-complexity BCI for robot swarm control. — [arXiv 2205.14265](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14265)
- Jedynak, Frazier & Sznitman, "Twenty questions with noise: Bayes optimal policies for entropy loss," J. Appl. Prob. 49(1):114–136, 2012. The target is found by repeatedly asking whether it lies in a chosen set, with noisy answers. Reported theorem: any policy that maximizes one-step expected entropy reduction is optimal over the full horizon (entropy loss). Two Bayes-optimal policies are given: a generalized Horstein probabilistic bisection and a deterministic set of questions. — [Cambridge Core](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/twenty-questions-with-noise-bayes-optimal-policies-for-entropy-loss/6F51F7E5CE7D0B1ED4CEF0221D0B6E08); [author PDF](https://www.cis.jhu.edu/~bruno/JedynakPaper-02-2011.pdf)
- Extensions: collaborative and decentralized noisy 20 questions. — [arXiv 1306.1922](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.1922); [arXiv 1312.7847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.7847)
- Roark et al., "Scanning methods and language modeling for binary switch typing," NAACL-HLT SLPAT Workshop 2010. Key issues include Huffman versus simpler binary coding trees and handling selection error inside the model. Reported qualitatively: Huffman scanning with an LM needs far fewer keystrokes than row/column scanning, gives faster text entry and is strongly preferred by users. — [ACL W10-1304](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/W10-1304.pdf); [PMC4617344](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4617344)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Williamson & Murray-Smith ("Hex," 2005; "Pointing without a pointer," 2004; later "interaction as control/inference" work) treat interaction as continuous inference over intention under noisy input. Not checked this session.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** RSVP Keyboard (Orhan, Erdogmus, Fried-Oken, OHSU/Northeastern) and BCI spellers with LM priors combine EEG evidence with an n-gram LM by Bayesian fusion. Not checked this session.

### Inferences
- The Jedynak greedy-optimality result suggests a usable baseline for our k=4 design. At each step, choose the 4-way partition of the LLM's intent posterior that maximizes one-step mutual information through the 4-ary noisy gesture channel. This is noisy-channel-aware Huffman or arithmetic partitioning, not "show top-4 actions." With top-4, any intent outside the top 4 needs a "none/more" escape, so a k=4 partition design should reserve or merge an "other" bucket (as KnowNO does, see below).
- The theorem is for entropy loss on a fixed hypothesis set. Our hypothesis set (all possible next actions or subgoals) is generated by the LLM and changes after each step. That violates the stationarity assumptions, so optimality does not carry over directly (inference).
- Under a symmetric 4-ary channel with 4% error, the ceiling is about 1.5 bits/stroke (estimate above). An undo or ErrP erasure mechanism could raise the effective rate toward 2 bits.

### Gaps
- Exact BIGnav, BIGFile and posterior-matching-BCI bits-per-selection and speed-up numbers were not extracted.
- I found no published Dasher- or BIG-style interface where the "alphabet" is LLM-proposed agent actions in a GUI.

## LLM-generated option sets, clarifying questions and information-gain asking

### Takeaway
2023–2026 LLM work optimizes when and what to ask: EIG-trained question generation for 20 questions, EIG/PMI-rewarded clarification in tool-using agents, and conformal multiple-choice option sets with a "none of the above" choice (KnowNO). Reported gains are modest but real. None of it optimizes a k-option menu for expected interaction length under a noisy discrete selection channel.

### Cited Findings
- Mazzaccara, Testoni & Bernardi, "Learning to Ask Informative Questions: Enhancing LLMs with Preference Optimization and Expected Information Gain," Findings of EMNLP 2024. LLMs, especially open-source ones, ask questions with low expected information gain in 20 questions. They sample several questions per game from LLaMA-2-Chat-7B, build low/high-EIG pairs and train with DPO. Reported: more effective questions, including in domains not used for training. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.291/); [arXiv 2406.17453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17453)
- Deng et al. (HKUST-GZ / Jilin), "Uncertainty-Aware Clarification in LLM Agents with Information Gain," arXiv 2606.03135 (June 2026, preprint). The clarifier is trained by policy optimization with an information-gain reward: the shift in length-normalized teacher-forced log-likelihood of the ground-truth goal after the question and answer (a PMI form). Abstaining earns zero reward. On τ-Bench (500 retail training trajectories; 115 held-out retail and 50 airline test tasks; five backbones), reported success rose by 3.7 points on average (27.3% vs 23.6%), with about 0.3 extra steps (1.2 vs 2.6 clarification turns for standard models) and up to +6.4 points for the 671B model. — [arXiv 2606.03135](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03135v1)
- SAGE-Agent, "Structured Uncertainty Guided Clarification for LLM Agents." It picks clarifying questions by expected value of information over tool parameters. Reported: 7–39% higher coverage with 1.5–2.7× fewer questions (search-result summary, primary page not read). — [project page](https://manansuri.com/papers/sage-clarification/)
- Ren et al., "Robots That Ask For Help: Uncertainty Alignment for Large Language Model Planners" (KnowNo), CoRL 2023, PMLR 229:661–682. The LLM generates candidate next-step plans as a multiple-choice question, including an "E: none of the above" option. Conformal prediction gives a calibrated prediction set. If the set is a singleton the robot acts; otherwise it asks the human to choose. This yields statistical task-completion guarantees while minimizing help. Reported qualitatively: favorable efficiency and autonomy against ensemble and prompt-tuning baselines. Help-rate numbers were not extracted. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html); [arXiv 2307.01928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928v1)
- Magentic-UI (Microsoft Research, 2025) is an open-source human-in-the-loop web/computer agent interface. It has six interaction mechanisms, including co-planning, co-tasking, action guards (approval before sensitive actions) and long-term memory. — [MSR report PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/magentic-ui-report.pdf)
- "Choose among options" interrupts (the agent presents alternatives and the human picks one) are a common agent-framework pattern in practice (industry documentation, not peer-reviewed). — [CopilotKit HITL explainer](https://www.copilotkit.ai/learning/what-is-human-in-the-loop); [AWS Bedrock HITL confirmation](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/implement-human-in-the-loop-confirmation-with-amazon-bedrock-agents/)
- Other related items surfaced but not read: Socratic questioning to close the instruction "expression gap" (arXiv 2510.27410). — [arXiv 2510.27410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.27410)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Diverse or set-level suggestion work (e.g., determinantal point process diversification; "set-level" or slate recommendation; pass@k-style coverage of LLM samples) supports choosing k items to maximize the probability that at least one is correct, as opposed to ranking by marginal probability. Specific papers were not verified this session.

### Inferences
- KnowNo is the closest existing mechanism to our per-step menu: LLM-generated MCQ, an explicit "none" escape and calibrated uncertainty to decide whether to ask. Its options are top-ranked plans, not designed to maximize information under a noisy chooser, and its human channel is assumed noiseless.
- The τ-Bench clarification gains (about +4 points) suggest that intent ambiguity is a real but not dominant failure source in agent benchmarks. Our efficiency gains should be measured as strokes-per-task and task success under simulated 4% gesture noise, not only success.
- A principled objective for our option set: maximize I(intent; selected gesture) under the 4-ary noisy channel, or directly minimize expected remaining strokes (dynamic program, with greedy EIG as a Jedynak-motivated approximation). This is subject to one option being "other/refine," and to irreversible actions never sharing a bucket with reversible ones.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that optimizes the composition of k LLM-proposed actions for a computer-use agent to minimize expected user selections under a noisy discrete channel.
- KnowNo help-rate numbers and SAGE-Agent primary-source numbers were not verified.

## Error handling with noisy selection (ErrP correction, undo, risk-aware confirmation)

### Takeaway
Automatic error detection works in BCI spellers. ErrP detection reached about 89% single-trial accuracy and raised spelling speed by about 49%, with the largest benefit for low-accuracy users. Automatic correction beat re-spelling. Decision-theoretic confirmation (ask when expected cost of false acceptance exceeds the cost of asking) appears in LLM-robot work as a calibrated "ask for help" rule and in agent products as action guards. A formal cost-weighted confirmation threshold for irreversible GUI actions under a noisy channel was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Schmidt, Blankertz & Treder, "Online detection of error-related potentials boosts the performance of mental typewriters," BMC Neuroscience 13:19, 2012. Gaze-independent visual speller, 11 participants. Reported: single-trial ErrP detection mean accuracy 89.1% (AUC 0.90). Spelling speed increased on average by 49.0% with ErrP detection, with the largest gains for participants with low spelling accuracy. — [PMC3315432](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3315432/)
- Related ErrP-speller work reports that automatic correction gave a higher bit rate than re-spelling, with about 4% higher spelling accuracy in a high-specificity group (search snippet, primary source not opened; plausibly Spüler et al. 2012). — [search result: Frontiers/related](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2022.984254/pdf)
- ErrPs can also flag errors made by predictive-text auto-correction, i.e., errors made by the system's prior, not by the user's input. — [SFU paper PDF](https://vvise.iat.sfu.ca/user/data/papers/classifyAutoCorrectEEG.pdf)
- KnowNo's conformal "ask when the prediction set is not a singleton" gives a calibrated confirmation rule with coverage guarantees. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html)
- BALI asks only when expected information gain exceeds interruption cost, an explicit cost-benefit query rule. — [arXiv 2512.04453](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04453)
- Magentic-UI "action guards" require user approval before designated sensitive actions. — [MSR report PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/magentic-ui-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Our wristband has no ErrP channel, but an "undo" gesture or a dedicated reject stroke plays the same role as an erasure signal. The ErrP results imply that error detection is worth most when per-step accuracy is lower. At 96%, gains will be smaller than the 49% in Schmidt et al., where speller accuracy was lower.
- Decision-theoretic confirmation (our proposal): execute an irreversible action only if P(intent = a | strokes) × U(correct) − (1 − P) × C(irreversible error) > cost of one more confirming stroke. A 4% per-stroke error means a single unconfirmed stroke has a ≥4% false-accept rate. Two concordant strokes reduce channel-induced false accept to about 0.04 × 0.013 ≈ 0.05% (estimate, assuming independent symmetric errors).

### Gaps
- No primary-source decision-theoretic confirmation threshold study for irreversible GUI or agent actions was found this session.
- No published numbers on undo design for noisy-input computer-use agents were found.

## Implicit feedback (accept/undo, outcomes) as pseudo-labels to adapt the input decoder

### Takeaway
BCI has validated self-calibration from task-inferred labels. Retrospective target inference in BrainGate used the user's own selections to relabel recent neural data, which preserved performance for hours and improved typing speed. ErrP-driven RL adaptation is an established research line. This maps directly to using accepted options (and undos as negative labels) to fine-tune the gesture decoder.

### Cited Findings
- Jarosiewicz et al., "Virtual typing by people with tetraplegia using a self-calibrating intracortical brain-computer interface," Science Translational Medicine, 2015 (not 2013). Three components: tracking neural statistics during self-timed pauses, velocity-bias correction, and periodic recalibration using movement intentions inferred retrospectively from the user's self-selected targets. Reported: performance was preserved much longer, accurate typing speed improved, and users typed for hours without recalibration pauses or technician intervention. — [PMC4765319](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4765319); [Brown Engineering news](https://engineering.brown.edu/news/2015-11-11/self-calibration-enhances-braingate-ease-reliability)
- "Retrospectively supervised click decoder calibration for self-calibrating point-and-click BCIs" extends this to the discrete click decoder. — [Einstein/Elsevier Pure](https://einstein.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/retrospectively-supervised-click-decoder-calibration-for-self-cal); [PMC5591042](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5591042)
- Chavarriaga, Ferrez & Millán proposed BCI adaptation from user error feedback. Later RL frameworks use an interaction-ErrP classifier as reward to adapt the BCI. A 2025 Frontiers study reports RL agents learning control policies from ErrP feedback with robust cross-dataset performance. — [Frontiers Hum. Neurosci. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1569411/full); [Llera et al. 2010 PDF](https://www.mbfys.ru.nl/staff/v.gomez/Llera10_final.pdf); [Frontiers Neurosci. 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2014.00208/pdf)
- ErrP amplitude encodes the magnitude of errors and individual perceptual thresholds (EPFL). — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/d32d3337068f419d97322d3d61ffab9e)

### Inferences
- For our system, the label available after each step is the option the user ultimately confirmed (and any undo). As with retrospective target inference, earlier strokes in that step can be relabelled to the confirmed target. This applies only when the user is assumed to have intended it, so label noise from user mind-changes must be handled.
- Our project rules require that adaptation be evaluated with held-out recordings, compared with shuffled-label controls, and checked for prediction diversity. Self-labelling loops can collapse onto frequent classes.

### Gaps
- "CORP" (named in the brief) was not identified or verified this session. Which method this acronym refers to remains unknown.
- No primary source was found that uses agent accept/undo signals to adapt an EMG or wristband gesture decoder.

## What has not been done (gap analysis)

### Takeaway
Each building block exists separately: shared autonomy with goal posteriors, EIG/BIG and noisy 20-questions selection, Dasher/Huffman LM-prior coding over tiny alphabets, LLM option sets with "none" and calibrated asking, ErrP/undo correction and retrospective self-calibration. I found no published system that combines an LLM computer-use agent prior, a noisy 4-symbol wearable gesture channel, and explicit design of the k=4 option set to minimize expected strokes, with risk-aware confirmation, for general GUI tasks.

### Cited Findings
- Shared autonomy assumes continuous control input and fixed goal sets, or LLM-generated goal sets for manipulation (BALI), not discrete menus for GUI agents. — [Javdani IJRR](https://personalrobotics.cs.washington.edu/publications/javdani2018shared.pdf); [BALI](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04453)
- Selection-interface theory (BIGnav, posterior matching, Jedynak) works over fixed target spaces (maps, files, letters or a continuum). — [BIGnav](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/bignav-bayesian-information-gain-for-guiding-multiscale-navigatio/); [Jedynak 2012](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/twenty-questions-with-noise-bayes-optimal-policies-for-entropy-loss/6F51F7E5CE7D0B1ED4CEF0221D0B6E08)
- LLM option or clarification work assumes a noiseless human response and optimizes question informativeness or when to ask, not menu composition under channel noise. — [KnowNo](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html); [Deng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03135v1)

### Inferences
- Specific novelty claims we could defend (pending a broader search): (1) channel-aware k-option design over LLM action posteriors; (2) an expected-strokes objective with an explicit "other" bucket and irreversible-action segregation; (3) cost-weighted multi-stroke confirmation; (4) using confirmed options to self-calibrate the gesture decoder.
- The main competitor baselines to beat in simulation: top-4 by LLM probability; KnowNo-style conformal set plus "none"; Huffman/Dasher-style partition of the posterior ignoring noise; plain EIG-greedy partition (Jedynak).

### Gaps
- The search was limited to about 20 tool calls. CHI/UIST 2024–2026 work on LLM-assisted AAC (e.g., LLM phrase prediction for ALS users) and EMG-wristband-controlled agents (e.g., Meta neuromotor wristband papers) was not searched here and may contain partial overlaps.
- The absence of prior work combining all components is a search-based judgement, not a proof.

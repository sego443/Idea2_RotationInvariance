# Four swipes steer agents: novelty lies in the coupling

No published system or study found in this review lets a person steer a general LLM computer-use agent with a handful of noisy wrist gestures. Every major ingredient does exist somewhere, but in a separate literature. Low-bandwidth users already pick among LLM-generated *replies* (Synchron, Neuralink, ChatBCI, SpeakFaster). Agents already propose next steps that humans accept, reject or override (CowPilot, Magentic-UI, Morae). The theory of choosing what to offer a noisy, low-rate chooser is decades old: shared autonomy, Dasher, BIGnav, posterior matching and Jedynak's noisy twenty questions. LLM multiple-choice menus with a "none of the above" escape and calibrated asking also exist (KnowNo, BALI). BCIs have a validated way to self-calibrate decoders from the user's own selections (retrospective target inference, CORP). What has not been found is the coupling: an agent's action distribution used as the prior, a measured 4×4 gesture confusion matrix as the likelihood, a menu composed to minimize expected strokes rather than ranked by probability, confirmation gated by both decoder uncertainty and action reversibility, and accept/undo outcomes fed back to recalibrate a cheap wearable after each rewear. The coupling is the defensible contribution. No single piece survives as a "first". The biggest threats are three. Meta's Neural Band is a shipped platform that could add swipe-to-select over agent steps at any time. Jedynak's theorem makes greedy information-gain menus a known-good baseline rather than a new idea. And no benchmark reports how often the correct next action is even in an agent's top four, so the whole idea rests on a number the project has to measure itself. The project figures used throughout (about 96% per-stroke accuracy after 4–8 calibration strokes, about 0.55 zero-calibration macro-F1 after rewearing) come from the project, were not re-verified in this review, and are single-participant, presegmented and same-session.

## LLM suggestions reach low-bandwidth users as replies, never as GUI actions

The closest industrial analogues are implant companies, and all of them stop at communication. In July 2024 Synchron announced an OpenAI-powered feature that "can now generate automated prompts for texting and chatting, categorized by contextual inputs, including the user's emotion". A participant with ALS selects these prompts with the Stentrode's point-and-click output ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/synchron-integrates-openais-ai-into-brain-computer-interface/)). In 2025 Synchron became the first device to use Apple's BCI HID profile. Its public iPad demo drove the OS through **Switch Control**, which scans UI elements one at a time. It does not choose among semantic actions ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/als-patient-controls-ipad-thought-using-synchron-and-apple-bci-tech); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/08/04/als-sufferer-uses-ipad-in-demonstration-of-thought-control)). Synchron's "Chiral" neural foundation model and its NVIDIA Holoscan smart-home demo come with no paper or benchmark ([eWEEK](https://www.eweek.com/de/news/synchron-chiral-brain-ai-medical-test/)). Neuralink integrated ChatGPT and Grok to "serve up a selection of relevant replies" that participant Brad Smith picks by brain-click. The press account gives no speed or accuracy numbers ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/05/07/1116139/this-brain-implant-gets-a-boost-from-generative-ai/)). All of these are **company demos with no published evaluation of the LLM layer**. None reports options per turn, acceptance rates or undo handling.

Peer-reviewed and preprint work fills in the pieces without assembling them. Lee et al. paired an EEG decoder with a camera-based **AI copilot** that infers the user's goal under shared autonomy. The paralyzed participant performed **3.9 times better** on a cursor task, and finished a robotic-arm block task in about 6.5 minutes that he could not finish without the copilot. The copilot is not described as an LLM, and N = 4 ([UCLA Samueli](https://samueli.ucla.edu/ai-co-pilot-boosts-noninvasive-brain-computer-interface-by-interpreting-user-intent/); [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01090-y)). ChatBCI (a preprint) put GPT-3.5 word suggestions into a P300 matrix and reports **80.68% keystroke savings** in improvised spelling (N = 7) ([arXiv 2411.15395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15395)). NOIR (CoRL 2023) lets EEG users select objects, skills and parameters from a fixed 14-skill robot library, and learns their choices few-shot. That adapts the *policy*, not the signal decoder ([arXiv 2311.01454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01454)). SpeakFaster (Nature Communications 2024) is the strongest peer-reviewed LLM-for-AAC result. LLM abbreviation expansion saved **57% more motor actions** than predictive keyboards in simulation and raised two ALS eye-gaze users' entry rates by **29–60%** ([PMC11530652](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11530652/)). Its authors suggest extending the approach to switch scanning, but it remains a text system. Voice-driven accessibility agents such as DexAssist cover web navigation for motor-impaired users without any discrete selection channel ([arXiv 2411.12214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12214)).

Two 2026 preprints matter most for the design, because both argue that decoder uncertainty must be enforced outside the LLM. Gorenshtein et al. replayed **1,065 P300-speller episodes from 47 people with ALS** through five LLM agents that had execute and abstain tools. No LLM reduced unfaithful execution below a deterministic confidence threshold at matched coverage. The apparent "safety gains" came from acting less often. A hybrid in which the LLM proposes and an external gate decides extended coverage in 5 of 10 models ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.13.26362955v1.full)). Tai's route-safety audit sent decoded binary EEG commands to a tool-using LLM. An independent confirmation window with split-conformal calibration cut the attack route rate **from 1.000 to 0.178 at FAR = 0.05**. Dual-decoder agreement alone was bypassed ([arXiv 2606.09315](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09315v1)). Both studies are offline, and both use EEG data rather than live users. A NeurIPS 2025 position paper calls for "Brain-Agent Collaboration" with agents that make up for low information-transfer rates, but it builds nothing ([arXiv 2510.22095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22095)).

| System | Evidence | Input | What the AI offers | Evaluation (reported) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Across this whole family, the pattern "LLM proposes, user picks" shows up only where the input is tiny: a click, a switch or a speller. High-bandwidth speech BCIs do not need it. That supports the premise. What remains open is the step from replies to agent-proposed *GUI actions*. The table's sources are cited in the paragraphs above.

## Shipped wrist gestures only navigate, and agent oversight assumes a perfect keyboard

Commercial wearables give their gestures small, safe roles. Meta's Neural Band, an sEMG wristband sold with Ray-Ban Display, uses "thumb swipes or subtle hand gestures" to "move through each stage" of a Meta AI response, and wrist rotation for volume ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/meta-ray-ban-display-ai-glasses-launch/)). The underlying Nature paper is the benchmark for calibration-free wrist input. Without per-user training it reports a closed-loop median of **0.88 discrete gesture detections per second** and **20.9 WPM handwriting**, with personalization improving handwriting by 16% ([CSHL repository PDF](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/42049/1/10.1038.s41586-025-09255-w.pdf)). That generalization comes from 16 bipolar sEMG channels and training data from thousands of participants. A low-cost 4-channel piezo band sits at a cheaper, lower-vocabulary point in the same design space and should be framed that way, not as an accuracy competitor. Apple's watchOS 26 Wrist Flick dismisses notifications and calls using motion sensors and an ML model ([heise](https://heise.de/-10491847)). Whether double tap can advance Siri or Apple Intelligence responses is **unverified**. Galaxy XR integrates Gemini with pinch, gaze and voice ([Samsung support](https://ushl.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10007511)). AI rings such as Natura's Interface give "one press" access to agents, but the task itself is spoken ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-natura-interface-ai-smart-ring-agents-price-features/)). Research prototypes follow the same split. The Columbia and Microsoft Research ring (CHI '26 extended abstract) uses a tap to activate, a whispered command, and a haptic signal when the agent is done ([MSR PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/chi26d-sub1595-cam-i61.pdf)). VisionClaw runs agents on Meta glasses from speech plus camera (lab N = 12) ([arXiv 2604.03486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03486)). No product or prototype found lets a gesture choose among agent-proposed actions.

Human-in-the-loop agent research has settled on the "agent proposes, human approves or overrides" pattern. All of it assumes full keyboard, mouse or screen-reader input. In CowPilot, on 27 WebArena tasks, GPT-4o rose from **48% autonomous to 95% in copilot mode**, against 89% for humans alone. Humans performed **15.2% of steps**, about 1.1 steps per task. The evaluators were three of the study's authors, and no timing was reported ([CowPilot](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16609v2)). CowCorpus logged 400 real-user trajectories with more than 4,200 interleaved actions. It found four interaction styles and intervention intensities of **21.63% on standard tasks and 16.06% on free-form ones** ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17588)). Its deployed PlowPilot's usefulness gain appears as 36.8% in one version and 26.5% in another, so cite a specific version ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17588)). Morae (UIST 2025) pauses UI agents at choice points for blind and low-vision users. Its verification questions take four answers: yes, no, unanswerable-and-proceed, and not-important-and-proceed. In a field study, users completed more tasks than with baselines that included OpenAI Operator ([arXiv 2508.21456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21456)). Morae's four-response scheme is the nearest published analogue to a four-symbol response vocabulary, though it was designed for screen-reader users, not for a noisy channel. Magentic-UI adds co-planning and **action guards**, which require approval before potentially irreversible actions at a frequency the user sets ([arXiv 2507.22358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22358)). Commercial agents do the same. Operator hands control to the user for credentials and payments and confirms purchases ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/01/23/openai-operator-agent-preview-chatgpt)). Project Mariner confirms sensitive actions ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/technologies/project-mariner/)). Anthropic's documentation advises asking a human to confirm "decisions that might result in meaningful real-world consequences" ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)). Several agents generate numbered candidate-action lists internally (WebChallenger, ANCHOR, PrecogUI), but the *model* picks from them, not a human ([arXiv 2606.10423](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10423); [arXiv 2602.07153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07153)).

The oversight literature brings a sobering result, and it reshapes the confirmation component. In a controlled study of computer-use agents (N = 48, 192 live sessions, a preprint), users blocked only **29 of 138 problematic actions that reached execution (21%)**, with no reliable difference across four oversight strategies. Plan-level strategies cut problematic-action occurrence by **76% (OR = 0.24)** by preventing problems upstream, not by catching them better. Approvals were often requested too late, or carried over into steps nobody anticipated ([arXiv 2604.04918](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04918v2)). A CHI '26 study (N = 30) found that users want to step in near sensitive actions or when ambiguity rises, and that agents asked for unnecessary confirmations too often. Only a search snippet was read ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772318.3791536)). Levels-of-autonomy frameworks treat "approver" as a design choice ([arXiv 2506.12469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12469v1)), but none of them models the reliability of the approval channel itself. That is the gap a noisy wristband exposes. A misclassified "confirm" adds channel error on top of a human catch rate that is already low, and no study found measures this compounding. One implication follows as inference: undo and upstream shaping of what gets offered should carry most of the safety burden. Per-step confirmation should be kept for actions that are actually irreversible.

## Noisy-channel selection theory is mature, but its alphabets were fixed

Shared autonomy supplies the framing. Dragan and Srinivasa split assistance into intent prediction and arbitration, and found that arbitration should depend on confidence ([CMU RI PDF](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/2013/5/assistive_teleop_IJRR.pdf)). Javdani et al. modelled the user's goal as a POMDP belief and showed that **acting on the full belief beats committing to the most likely goal**. They reported faster goal reaching and less user input, though users split on preference because of a trade-off between control and speed ([arXiv 1706.00155](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/1706.00155)). Reddy et al. dropped the explicit goal model and used deep RL, with significantly more successful Lunar Lander landings for human plus copilot (N = 12) ([arXiv 1802.01744](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.01744)). BCI arm work by Muelling et al. and Downey et al. made infeasible grasping tasks feasible for tetraplegic users ([arXiv 1503.05451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.05451); [PMC4797113](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4797113/)). In all of these the user's signal is continuous velocity and autonomy fills in motor detail, so their gains do not carry over numerically to discrete menu steps. Javdani's result, though, argues directly for **set-level menu design over plain top-4 ranking**. The menu should be chosen to serve the whole intent posterior, not to list the four largest marginals. The closest LLM-era robotics analogue is BALI (HRI 2026). An LLM maintains open-ended goal hypotheses scored against actions and language, and asks a clarifying question only when expected information gain exceeds the cost of interrupting ([arXiv 2512.04453](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04453)). Its quantitative gains were not extracted.

Information-efficient selection interfaces provide the objective. Dasher's inverse arithmetic coding let users write **up to 25 WPM by gaze, against 15 WPM on an on-screen keyboard** with about five times more errors ([arXiv cs/0204030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0204030)). BIGnav keeps a posterior over the intended map target and moves to the view that maximizes the expected information gain of the user's next command. It reports significantly faster navigation than pan-and-zoom ([IP Paris record](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/bignav-bayesian-information-gain-for-guiding-multiscale-navigatio/)). Posterior matching treats the BCI as a feedback communication channel and uses capacity-achieving codes ([Coleman lab](https://colemanlab.stanford.edu/research/brain-machine-interface); [arXiv 0909.4828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.4828)). Jedynak, Frazier and Sznitman proved that in noisy twenty questions **any policy maximizing one-step expected entropy reduction is optimal over the full horizon** for entropy loss ([Cambridge Core](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/twenty-questions-with-noise-bayes-optimal-policies-for-entropy-loss/6F51F7E5CE7D0B1ED4CEF0221D0B6E08)). Huffman scanning with a language model needs far fewer switch presses than row-column scanning ([PMC4617344](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4617344)). Nomon learns each switch user's click-timing distribution and outperformed row-column scanning over 10 sessions ([arXiv 2204.01619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01619)). The proposed "choose the 4 options to minimize expected strokes" is therefore a k = 4 member of a known family. It is not a new principle. What is new is the hypothesis space. Here it is generated by an LLM over GUI actions and changes after every step, which breaks the fixed-hypothesis assumption behind Jedynak's optimality proof (inference). So greedy expected information gain is the baseline to beat, not a guarantee.

LLM work on option sets supplies the closest mechanism, and it assumes a noiseless human. KnowNo (CoRL 2023) poses next-step plans as a multiple-choice question with an explicit **"none of the above"** option. Conformal prediction then gives a calibrated set: the robot acts when the set is a singleton and asks the human otherwise ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html)). Its options are top-ranked plans, not chosen for a noisy chooser, and its help-rate numbers were not extracted. Information-gain clarification for tool-using agents lifts τ-Bench success modestly, **27.3% against 23.6%** on average, a preprint result ([arXiv 2606.03135](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03135v1)). That suggests intent ambiguity is a real but not dominant failure source. Preference optimization on expected information gain improves LLM twenty-questions play ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.291/)). Industry agent frameworks expose "choose among options" interrupts as a standard pattern, which is documentation and not research ([CopilotKit](https://www.copilotkit.ai/learning/what-is-human-in-the-loop)).

Error handling and self-calibration have direct BCI precedents. Online detection of error-related potentials reached **89.1% single-trial accuracy** and raised spelling speed by **49.0%**, with the largest gains for low-accuracy users (N = 11) ([PMC3315432](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3315432/)). The wristband has no ErrP channel. An explicit undo stroke plays the same erasure role, and at 96% per-stroke accuracy its benefit should be smaller than in lower-accuracy spellers (inference). For recalibration, Jarosiewicz et al. (Science Translational Medicine 2015) retrained an intracortical decoder from movement intentions **inferred retrospectively from the user's own selections**. Users typed for hours without recalibration pauses ([PMC4765319](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4765319)). The approach was later extended to the discrete click decoder ([PMC5591042](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5591042)). CORP used language-model-corrected output as pseudo-labels and held **93.84% accuracy over 403 days** ([NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/83a14a36de4502bac5b580db36e81858-Abstract.html)). ErrP-rewarded RL adaptation is an established line of work ([Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1569411/full)). "Use accepted options and undos as labels for the gesture decoder" is therefore retrospective target inference moved to a new setting. What no source found does is run it on a wearable after rewearing, with labels derived from an agent's task context.

Two quantities frame the design. Both are this review's own arithmetic, not reported values. First, a 4-ary symmetric channel at 96% accuracy has a capacity of about **1.69 bits per stroke** (2 − H(0.96, 0.0133, 0.0133, 0.0133) ≈ 2 − 0.31). One research note gives 1.5 bits, but it misstates the −0.96·log₂0.96 term. The agent's prior, not the channel, must carry most of the information. Second, without undo, gesture noise alone caps a 7-step task at 0.96⁷ ≈ 75% success even if the right action is always on the menu. With a reliably recognized undo, each step costs about 1 + 0.04 × 2 ≈ 1.08 strokes. Undo is therefore what makes the idea viable, not an extra. The same arithmetic shows a design tension in the brief. With exactly four gestures, any stroke reserved for reject, more or undo leaves only three real options on that screen. So the reserved meanings need modes, for example a separate confirm or undo screen, a repeated or multi-stroke code, or one option slot reserved for "other/refine", as KnowNo and Mind2Web's None option do. Two concordant strokes on an irreversible action would bring channel-induced false acceptance down to roughly 0.05%, assuming independent symmetric errors (estimate).

## Benchmarks support an offline simulation, but nobody reports top-four coverage

The quantity that decides whether the idea works is Pass@4: how often the correct next action is anywhere in four agent proposals. **No primary source reports it for a frontier computer-use agent.** The proxies give a range. Top-1 step success, reported in the UI-TARS paper, is **60–69% on Multimodal-Mind2Web, about 72–78% on AndroidControl-High, about 87–91% on AndroidControl-Low and about 87–89% on GUI-Odyssey** for fine-tuned UI-TARS models. Zero-shot frontier APIs without a grounding model often score below 20% in the same tables ([UI-TARS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12326)). So the choice of proposer dominates any simulation. Best-of-N curves show that sampled candidate sets contain the correct action more often than the greedy action, but they appear only in a figure ([UI-TARS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12326)). One 2026 mobile world-model paper reports **Pass@3 step accuracy of 69.55% on AITZ** for Qwen3-VL-8B. Its extracted Pass@9 value is lower, which is non-monotonic, so treat it with caution ([arXiv 2605.10347](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10347v1)). Element-retrieval recall is higher but measures something different: Mind2Web Recall@50 is 85–89% ([Mind2Web](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06070)) and WebLINX Recall@10 is 74% in-domain and about 52% out-of-domain ([WebLINX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05930v2)).

Mind2Web is the natural first testbed because it already runs a multiple-choice protocol. MindAct presents "up to 5 candidate elements … together with a None option" and runs a tournament over the top-ranked candidates. Each step is evaluated independently with the ground-truth history supplied. The 2,350 tasks average 7.3 actions, and the data are CC BY 4.0 ([Mind2Web](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06070)). Mind2Web's own GPT-4 evaluation used 50 tasks per split "due to limited budget", a precedent for cheap subsets. AndroidControl (Apache 2.0, 15,283 demonstrations) pairs each step with a human low-level instruction, so a Low-versus-High contrast separates "intent known" from "goal only" ([AndroidControl repo](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/android_control)). Online environments show error propagation and real undo. They include OSWorld-Verified (human 72.36% on the original task set; about 20-minute parallel runs after migration) ([XLANG blog](https://xlang.ai/blog/osworld-verified); [OSWorld](http://osworld-v1.xlang.ai/)), AndroidWorld (116 tasks, human 80%) and CowPilot's 27-task WebArena subset ([CowPilot](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16609v2)). Aggregator leaderboards put 2026 systems at or above human baselines on OSWorld-Verified and AndroidWorld. Those figures are **unverified** against official leaderboards ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/osworld-verified)), and a February 2026 paper claims perfect AndroidWorld accuracy ([arXiv 2602.07787](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07787)). Near-saturation means a steering interface adds the most value with weaker or cheaper proposers, so results should be reported across proposer strength.

The simulation methodology has known traps that reviewers will raise. User simulation is an accepted evaluation tool ([Balog & Zhai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08550v1)), and τ-bench's LLM-simulated users and pass^k metric are a common template ([τ-bench](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045)). Teacher forcing hides error recovery, because a wrong pick never reaches a new state. A single ground truth undercounts alternative valid actions. A simulated user who always recognizes the correct option overstates gains. Neither Mind2Web nor AndroidControl labels irreversible actions, so an irreversible-error rate needs a rule-based label set (submit, purchase, send, delete). Mind2Web is also from 2023 and appears in many models' training data, which is a contamination risk. Time per task can combine KLM operator times for reading and deciding (M = 1.35 s) with the measured stroke time and agent latency ([KLM slides](https://www.medien.informatik.uni-muenchen.de/lehre/ws0708/mmi1/KLM.pdf)). No validated KLM extension exists for reading four agent proposals.

## Synthesis: what is done, what is defensible, what threatens it

**Already done, and to be cited rather than claimed.** Low-bandwidth users already select LLM-generated replies and word suggestions (Synchron, Neuralink, ChatBCI, SpeakFaster). An AI copilot already infers a BCI user's goal (Lee et al. 2025). Agents already propose steps that humans approve or override, with measured intervention rates of about 15–22% (CowPilot, CowCorpus, Morae, Magentic-UI). Action guards for irreversible actions are already a product feature. An LLM multiple-choice menu with a "none" option and conformal asking exists (KnowNo), as does expected-information-gain asking over LLM goal hypotheses (BALI). Bayes-optimal greedy question selection under noisy answers is proven (Jedynak), and information-gain interfaces exist (BIGnav, Dasher, posterior matching). Deterministic or conformal gates outside the LLM have been argued for (Gorenshtein, Tai), and self-calibration from inferred targets has been demonstrated (Jarosiewicz, CORP). Calibration-free wrist gestures already navigate AI responses in a shipped product (Meta).

**Novel and defensible**, phrased as "not found in our search" and never as "first". The first candidate is a menu-composition objective. It chooses the four options, plus a reserved escape, to minimize expected strokes to the intended action under a measured gesture confusion matrix, given an LLM agent's action posterior. That posterior is non-stationary and generated by the LLM, and no fixed-alphabet theory covers it. The second candidate is a confirmation policy computed outside the LLM from three inputs together: decoder posterior, agent confidence and action reversibility. It would be the first such policy evaluated where the confirm channel is itself error-prone. The third candidate is self-calibration of a low-cost wearable after rewearing, using agent-context pseudo-labels (accepted options as positives, undos as negatives) and measured as strokes to recover the calibrated confusion matrix. The fourth is the platform and the measurement: a passive 4-channel piezo band with in-air swipes driving a general GUI agent, evaluated with Pass@4, strokes per task and irreversible false accepts. That combination of measurements does not appear in any benchmark paper found. There is also a design idea that has not been tested and that parallels the earlier text-entry work. Right after a rewear, the agent could deliberately compose menus whose options differ sharply in prior probability, so that the user's choices identify the new gesture-to-direction mapping quickly. That would be active identification rather than passive pseudo-labelling.

**Threats to novelty and to the claims.** Meta could ship swipe-to-select over multi-step Meta AI flows at any time. That would be a product precedent, probably without published uncertainty modelling. Jedynak's theorem and BIGnav make greedy expected-information-gain menus an obvious baseline, so the contribution has to show a measurable gain from modelling the confusion matrix and the expected-strokes objective. Those gains could be small at 96% accuracy and appear only under post-rewear degradation. KnowNo plus Magentic-UI already cover "menu with none, plus guarded actions" for noiseless humans. Retrospective target inference already uses selections as labels. The wearable version also has a circularity the BCI version partly avoids: the user can only accept options the decoder and agent offered, so pseudo-labels are conditioned on the system's own outputs and can collapse onto frequent classes. Shuffled-label controls and diversity checks are mandatory. If Pass@4 is low for the chosen proposer, the agent prior cannot rescue a 1.7-bit channel. If it is very high, the problem shrinks to confirming top-1. The 21% human block rate means a gesture interface cannot claim to make oversight safe. Finally, coverage was limited. ASSETS, CHI, UIST and IMWUT 2025–2026 work on switch- or EMG-driven agents was not searched systematically. Apple double tap with Siri, Humane and Rabbit, ChatGPT agent's confirmation specifics, Operator system-card metrics and the Satori paper are all **unverified**. On the evidence side, the project's numbers are single-participant, presegmented and same-session, and the existing C170 test has been exposed.

**Baselines reviewers will expect.**

| Component | Expected baselines |
|---|---|
| Steering | Agent alone (top-1, no human); CowPilot-style full-input copilot as the upper reference; random picker over the same 4 options |
| Menu policy | Top-4 by LLM probability; deduplicated top-4 by (action type, element, value); KnowNo-style conformal set plus "none"; Huffman or Dasher partition ignoring channel noise; noise-aware greedy expected information gain (Jedynak); the proposed expected-strokes policy |
| Confirmation | Never; always; Magentic-UI-style guard on rule-labelled irreversible actions; a deterministic decoder-confidence threshold (Gorenshtein); conformal confirmation (Tai); the proposed reversibility × posterior rule |
| Channel | Measured confusion matrix at the calibrated level (about 96%) and at post-rewear levels; symmetric-noise and perfect-channel ablations |
| Recalibration | No update; 4–8 labelled strokes as the upper reference; hard pseudo-labels from accepts (retrospective target inference, CORP-style); accepts plus undo negatives; unlabeled re-centering; shuffled-pseudo-label and matched training-block permutation controls with per-class recall and prediction diversity |
| Proposer | At least one weak and one strong proposer, with in-domain and cross-domain splits reported separately |

**Recommended first simulation (a design, not yet run).** Run it offline on a 50–100-task subset per Mind2Web test split, teacher-forced. A zero-shot API proposer returns several deduplicated candidate actions per step, and each menu policy picks which four to show. Measure Pass@1 and Pass@4 first, because every downstream conclusion depends on them. The simulated user picks the ground-truth option if it is shown and "other" if not. The pick passes through the project's measured 4×4 confusion matrix, undo is modelled analytically, and a human-judgement error rate is swept. Metrics are step and task success, strokes per task, KLM plus measured-stroke time per task, and the rate of misclassified picks that land on rule-labelled irreversible actions. Run the matrix at calibrated and post-rewear quality. Add the pseudo-label recalibration loop with a shuffled-label control, and report strokes until the confusion matrix recovers. Then repeat on AndroidControl Low and High, and only after that on a small online WebArena or AndroidWorld subset to observe real error propagation. Rough scale (estimate): a full Mind2Web test pass is about 9,800 steps, so subsets keep the first run cheap. The first experiment needs no new recordings. It reuses existing confusion matrices, and must not reuse the exposed C170 test for any confirmatory claim.

**Recommended positioning.** Frame the work as *a noisy-channel selection interface that uses a computer-use agent as its prior*. That is information-efficient selection (Dasher, BIGnav, Jedynak) extended to LLM-generated, non-stationary action spaces, with safety enforced by a decision-theoretic gate outside the LLM, and with the agent's context paying back the wearable's calibration cost. Present CowPilot, Morae and Magentic-UI as the interaction precedent, KnowNo and BALI as the closest option-set and asking mechanisms, Jarosiewicz and CORP as the self-calibration lineage, and Meta's band as the high-cost alternative that buys calibration-freedom with data. Lead with the expected-strokes and confirmation results under realistic channel noise, and keep the rewear self-calibration as the distinctive second contribution. Do not claim continuous gesture detection, cross-person or cross-day robustness, or safety guarantees beyond what the simulation and any later user study actually show.

## Conclusion

Set against the earlier text-entry review, the computer-control variant changes where the information comes from. In text entry, a four-symbol code was already occupied by FlexType and LowKeyEMG, and the defensible core was language naming a permuted code after rewearing. Here the agent's prior over the next action carries far more of the load, but it does so over a hypothesis space that nobody has fixed or measured. So the critical unknown is empirical, not algorithmic: whether an agent's top four contains the user's intent often enough for a 1.7-bit channel to close the gap. Measuring that Pass@4 is a modest first contribution in its own right, and it decides whether menu optimization, confirmation design or recalibration matters most.

The second shift is about safety. The oversight studies show that humans with perfect keyboards stop only about a fifth of the bad actions that reach execution. A noisy wristband cannot fix that by confirming more. Its value has to come from offering better options upstream, making undo cheap, and keeping irreversibility decisions out of both the LLM and the classifier's single stroke. If the simulation shows that confusion-aware menus beat noise-blind information-gain menus mainly after rewearing, then calibration and steering turn out to be one problem. That would give the cleanest version of the paper.
